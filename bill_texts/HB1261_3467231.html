By: Spencer H.B. No. 1261
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the exercise of eminent domain for a demonstrated
  public necessity; limiting certain condemnation powers; requiring
  independent review, public hearings and county elections, enhanced
  compensation, repurchase rights, and revenue accounting; providing
  court review, injunctive relief, recovery of costs, and
  enforcement; contingent in part on a constitutional amendment.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  SHORT TITLE. This Act may be cited as the Texas
  Private Property and Public Necessity Protection Act.
         SECTION 2.  LEGISLATIVE PURPOSE. The legislature finds that
  involuntary acquisition of private property is an extraordinary
  governmental act, that a public benefit is not by itself proof of an
  indispensable need for a particular parcel, and that the public and
  landowners require transparent findings, genuine alternatives,
  meaningful local participation, adequate compensation, and
  judicial review before property is taken.
         SECTION 3.  Chapter 2206, Government Code, is amended by
  adding Subchapter E to read as follows:
  SUBCHAPTER E. STATEWIDE PUBLIC NECESSITY STANDARDS
         Sec. 2206.201.  DEFINITIONS. In this subchapter:
               (1)  "Condemning authority" means any governmental or
  private person or entity asserting eminent-domain power under a
  general, local, or special law, including an agency, municipality,
  county, special district, water supply corporation, public
  utility, railway, common carrier, corporation created or
  controlled by a governmental entity, or agent acting on behalf of
  another entity.
               (2)  "Covered project" means the entire undertaking for
  which property acquisition by eminent domain is proposed, including
  related reservoirs, roads, pipelines, power facilities,
  transmission lines, mitigation areas, access facilities, storage,
  development phases, and improvements under common control or
  coordinated funding. A connected project may not be artificially
  divided to avoid a statutory requirement.
               (3)  "Public necessity" means an objectively
  demonstrated, immediate and critical need for essential public
  infrastructure or services directly benefiting the general public,
  including the local population where a taking occurs, that cannot
  reasonably be supplied by a feasible, less intrusive alternative.
  "Immediate" requires a presently established need and an
  objectively justified implementation schedule; speculative growth
  projections alone are insufficient. This definition does not limit
  a more protective standard in the Texas Constitution.
               (4)  "Feasible alternative" means a lawful,
  technically capable option reasonably implementable within an
  objectively justified period, including a combination of options,
  even if not the condemnor's least expensive or preferred option.
  Substantiated disproportionate cost and infeasibility may be
  considered; an avoidable or self-created deadline is not proof of
  necessity.
               (5)  "Protected land" means prime farmland identified
  under United States Department of Agriculture soil
  classifications, active timberland, watershed or recharge land,
  bottomland hardwood forest, a multigenerational ranch held
  continuously in family ownership for at least 25 years, or a
  homestead occupied or qualified for homestead treatment under
  applicable law. The claimant may supply reasonable records;
  absence of a tax exemption does not defeat otherwise qualifying
  land.
               (6)  "Affected owner" means a record title owner,
  including a cotenant group treated as one ownership unit for a
  particular parcel, whose land is sought through eminent domain for
  the covered project. Affiliates may not fabricate or consolidate
  owners to evade a threshold. A person owning multiple parcels is
  counted once for the separate-owner threshold.
               (7)  "Taking" includes acquiring fee title, easements,
  options secured by threatened condemnation, long-term exclusive
  occupancy, or another compensable property interest; the
  imposition of project-related restrictions may be examined under
  applicable takings law.
         Sec. 2206.202.  STATEWIDE APPLICATION; PREEMPTION OF STATE
  EXCEPTIONS. (a) This subchapter governs every proposed
  condemnation under state law by a public or private condemnor,
  regardless of size or location, and supplements Chapter 21,
  Property Code.
         (b)  Notwithstanding Section 2206.001(c), Section
  2206.053(f) or (g), or a conflicting general, special, or local
  law, a permit, franchise, common-carrier designation, certificate
  of convenience, water plan, declaration of public use, funding
  award, or vote of an appointed board does not dispense with this
  subchapter.
         (c)  The subchapter applies to transportation, water,
  wastewater, drainage, ports, public buildings, schools, parks,
  utilities, transmission, pipelines, storage facilities, and other
  undertakings alike, but confers no new condemnation authority. No
  private contract, joint venture, subsidiary, proxy, affiliate,
  interlocal agreement, route change, condemnation threat, or staged
  acquisition may evade the requirements.
         (d)  A federal agency's exercise of independent federal
  eminent-domain authority is not regulated by this state Act. Its
  application to a federally certificated or preempted project is
  limited to the extent consistent with controlling federal law; a
  condemnor invoking preemption shall identify the specific federal
  authority in a verified public filing before asserting the
  exception. No broader exception is implied.
         Sec. 2206.203.  PROHIBITED PURPOSES AND NONDELEGATION. (a)
  A taking is prohibited if its primary purpose is speculative
  population or tax-base growth, private development, private
  commerce, increased private profit, assembly for resale, or a
  public-private venture principally supplying private economic
  return. A subsequent private lease or transfer is evidence to be
  considered, but the economic substance controls.
         (b)  An entity created to profit from acquiring condemned
  property, a private for-profit company, or an unelected governing
  board may not hold or exercise a new state-law delegation of
  eminent-domain power after the effective date. Only the state or an
  elected political-subdivision governing body may authorize a
  taking; an elected authorizing body must consider each affected
  project and issue written parcel-specific findings. A ministerial
  filing by staff does not replace its vote.
         (c)  An entity with a preexisting claim of private
  condemnation authority is subject to the transition provisions of
  this Act, applicable constitutional law, and the express limits in
  this section. This section shall be administered consistently with
  Section 2206.215 and the Constitutions of Texas and the United
  States.
         Sec. 2206.204.  BURDEN OF PROOF; NECESSITY; ALTERNATIVES.
  (a) Before filing a condemnation petition or threatening
  involuntary possession, a condemnor shall prove by clear and
  convincing evidence for each tract and each interest sought that
  (1) the project meets a present public necessity; (2) the exact
  parcel and interest are indispensable to that necessity; (3) no
  feasible combination of conservation, design changes, routes,
  sites, technology, existing property, demand reduction, voluntary
  acquisition, or a lesser estate can meet the demonstrated need; and
  (4) the taking is the least intrusive reasonably feasible means.
         (b)  The purpose must be defined without reference to the
  preferred site or sponsor. Greater population, forecast taxes,
  grants, economic development, or the convenience of the sponsor
  does not create a presumption. An offer refused by an owner is not
  evidence of public necessity.
         (c)  The assessment shall quantify actual local, downstream,
  and surrounding economic, agricultural, ecological, environmental,
  access, drainage, cultural, community, and cumulative effects.
  Project-specific mitigation shall be evaluated but cannot excuse a
  prohibited taking or replace the necessity proof.
         (d)  A landowner is not required to retain an expert or
  design an alternative. Unresolved material uncertainty or
  unsupported assumptions are insufficient to satisfy the
  condemnor's burden.
         Sec. 2206.205.  INDEPENDENT REVIEW PANEL. (a) Before the
  public hearing required by Section 2206.206, the condemnor shall
  submit the complete project and parcel-specific necessity record to
  an independent three-member review panel appointed by the district
  court in the county where the property lies from an approved public
  roster of qualified independent professionals.
         (b)  One member must possess relevant engineering or
  infrastructure expertise; one must possess land valuation,
  agricultural, or environmental expertise appropriate to the
  affected property; and one must be an attorney experienced in
  property or administrative law or a qualified neutral with judicial
  experience. No member may have represented or received
  project-related compensation from the condemnor, its affiliates,
  or the acquiring beneficiary during the preceding five years.
         (c)  The panel shall review alternatives, demand and supply
  assumptions, local benefits, impacts, and the proposed
  acquisition. It shall conduct a publicly noticed evidentiary
  meeting, accept written and oral objections, and publish an
  independently reasoned recommendation with its data and dissenting
  conclusions, if any, within 90 days of a complete submission.
         (d)  The panel's recommendation is evidence but is not
  conclusive, creates no presumption, and does not replace judicial
  findings. The condemnor pays reasonable approved costs into a
  court-administered fund; affected owners pay no panel costs. The
  court may replace a conflicted member and may order supplementation
  of an inadequate record.
         Sec. 2206.206.  FULL DISCLOSURE; PUBLIC HEARINGS. (a) At
  least 90 days before filing a condemnation petition, the condemnor
  shall provide each affected owner, county commissioners court, and
  affected municipality a complete project description, each route or
  site alternative, demand evidence, land map and ownership list,
  independent-review report, environmental and economic impact
  assessments, parcel-specific necessity findings, survey limits,
  acquisition instrument, and written notice of rights.
         (b)  The documents shall be available without charge online
  and in a physical public location in each affected county. Notice
  shall be mailed to affected owners; published in an appropriate
  newspaper; and posted publicly at least 30 days before a recorded
  hearing in each affected county. The hearing must allow reasonable
  oral and written comment and address material objections in a
  written response before a vote.
         (c)  A confidentiality agreement, trade-secret designation,
  contract with an intermediary, or nondisclosure clause may not
  conceal information material to condemnation necessity from an
  owner or court. Information lawfully protected from the general
  public may be examined under a judicial protective order; it may not
  be relied on to establish necessity if withheld from the affected
  owner and judge.
         Sec. 2206.207.  COUNTYWIDE REFERENDUM. (a) Before a
  condemnor files the first petition for a covered project that
  proposes, in aggregate, to acquire through eminent domain more than
  100 acres or property of more than 10 separate owners, the project
  must receive an affirmative majority vote at a project-specific
  election in each county in which a project taking would occur.
         (b)  An affirmative vote is required in every affected
  county; approval in another county cannot substitute. A county
  commissioners court shall order the election when it receives a
  complete, verified application and a deposit of the reasonable
  election costs from the condemnor. An elected statewide entity is
  subject to the same county elections for projects under state
  authority.
         (c)  The proposition shall identify the project, maximum
  condemned acreage, general route or footprint, necessity claimed,
  beneficiaries, and maximum proposed interest to be taken, and shall
  ask whether the project may use eminent domain in that county. It
  may not package unrelated projects. Material expansion requires a
  new vote.
         (d)  Elections shall be administered under the Election
  Code, including Chapter 41 and applicable notice and canvassing
  laws. An election may not be held until after the independent panel
  report and county hearing. The condemnor shall bear documented
  election costs. Failure to hold an election is denial, not deemed
  approval.
         (e)  For determining thresholds, all reasonably foreseeable
  project phases, connected parcels, threatened condemnation
  acquisitions, common owners, affiliates, and substantially related
  routes or sites must be aggregated. An initial voluntary purchase
  that does not involve threatened condemnation is not itself
  condemned acreage, but a sham acquisition to evade this section
  shall be counted.
         Sec. 2206.208.  PROTECTED AGRICULTURAL AND NATURAL LANDS.
  (a) Land meeting Section 2206.201(5) may be condemned only on a
  showing by clear and convincing evidence of unavoidable immediate
  public necessity for that protected tract, as independently
  reviewed and judicially determined, and only after at least
  two-thirds of votes cast on the question by the qualified voters in
  each county containing affected protected land are in favor.
         (b)  This special election is required regardless of project
  acreage or number of affected owners. When both Sections 2206.207
  and 2206.208 apply, one ballot may be used only if it clearly states
  the heightened two-thirds threshold. No approval in one county
  overrules a no vote in another.
         (c)  A project sponsor must evaluate farmland, timberland,
  homestead, and watershed avoidance and demonstrate why an
  alternative route or site outside protected lands is infeasible.
  Future mitigation or payments to unrelated parties do not satisfy
  the prohibition.
         Sec. 2206.209.  PRIOR JUDICIAL HEARING; NO EARLY POSSESSION.
  (a) The district court where the tract is located has original
  jurisdiction, before appointment of special commissioners under
  Chapter 21, Property Code, to decide lawful authority, public
  necessity, independent panel compliance, elections, protected
  status, the validity of a proposed interest, and the absence of
  feasible alternatives.
         (b)  The condemnor must produce all evidence and establish
  each element by clear and convincing evidence. The court shall
  conduct an evidentiary hearing with reasonable discovery and
  opportunity for the owner to examine witnesses, make independent
  findings as to each parcel, and deny or dismiss a proceeding that
  fails the burden.
         (c)  Neither a permit nor a condemnor's own determination
  receives deference. No court or special commissioner may permit
  possession, entry for construction, site disturbance, or transfer
  of ownership pending a final favorable necessity determination and
  completion of timely review. Lawful preliminary surveys remain
  governed by existing law and do not authorize intrusive
  construction or a permanent property right.
         (d)  Either party may take an interlocutory appeal from the
  necessity ruling. The filing of a timely appeal stays all
  condemnation and possession activity for the affected parcel
  through resolution of that appeal. A deposit, bond, emergency
  declaration, or commissioners' award does not waive the stay.
         (e)  An owner who obtains dismissal or an injunction for
  noncompliance, or whose condemnor voluntarily dismisses after a
  substantial contest to necessity, shall receive reasonable
  attorney, expert, appraisal, and court costs. An unsuccessful
  owner shall not owe the condemnor's professional fees solely for
  raising a good-faith challenge.
         Sec. 2206.210.  COMPENSATION; DAMAGES AND COSTS. (a) The
  minimum amount for a taking permitted by this subchapter is 200
  percent of the fair market value of the interest condemned as of the
  valuation date immediately preceding any project-caused
  diminution, plus independently established severance damages to
  the remainder and reasonable actual relocation, loss-of-access
  mitigation, restoration, fencing, drainage,
  business-interruption, moving, and necessary appraisal, legal, and
  professional expenses caused by the taking.
         (b)  The 200 percent value floor applies separately to the
  interest taken. Additional categories may not be used to reduce
  that floor, and an item of actual loss may be recovered only once.
  This section provides an enhancement beyond ordinary
  constitutional compensation and does not reduce another remedy or
  federal minimum.
         (c)  The condemnor shall pay or secure the required amount
  before possession. If any owner contests valuation, the undisputed
  amount shall be tendered, but tender alone does not authorize
  possession while necessity remains in litigation. The court shall
  authorize independently obtained appraisals and allow evidence of
  long-term agricultural and timber use insofar as relevant under the
  appraisal law.
         (d)  For an easement, the acquired interest must be narrowly
  described by area, duration, permissible uses, access, future
  expansion, restoration duty, and termination; a condemnor may not
  treat a limited corridor award as blanket authority for unrelated
  future facilities.
         Sec. 2206.211.  10-YEAR RETURN; NO SHAM PROGRESS. (a) In
  addition to all rights under Subchapter E, Chapter 21, Property
  Code, a former owner or the owner's heirs, successors, or assigns
  has an enforceable right to repurchase an interest condemned under
  this subchapter if the property is not actually placed into the
  original specifically stated public-necessity use within 10 years
  after acquisition, even if surveying, planning, procurement,
  financing, permitting, or another preliminary act occurred.
         (b)  The right also arises earlier upon cancellation,
  abandonment, disposal, or change to a substantially different use.
  A redevelopment label, partial nonproductive use, transfer to an
  affiliate, or lease may not constitute use unless the condemned
  parcel is materially serving the authorized public necessity.
         (c)  The condemnor shall notify the former owner and known
  heirs promptly upon an event in Subsection (b) and not later than 30
  days after the 10-year deadline. The person has at least 180 days
  after actual notice to accept an offer of repurchase, and 180 days
  after acceptance to close. Failure to deliver notice does not
  extinguish the right.
         (d)  The repurchase price is the original compensation
  attributable to the interest reacquired, adjusted by the change in
  the United States Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers from
  acquisition through repurchase; the amount may not be increased for
  improvements installed solely for an abandoned purpose. The seller
  shall convey the same estate or interest reasonably capable of
  reconveyance, free of liens created by the condemnor.
         (e)  The district court may compel conveyance, rescind an
  unlawful transfer, and award costs and reasonable fees to a
  prevailing former owner. The stronger of this section and
  otherwise applicable repurchase law governs a new covered
  acquisition. Existing lawful repurchase rights are not diminished.
         Sec. 2206.212.  DIRECT PROPERTY REVENUE RETURN. (a) If
  receipts or revenue are obtained directly from property
  involuntarily acquired for a covered project, including leases,
  license fees, royalties, rents, sales, commercial access charges,
  concessions, or consideration for later transfer or commercial use,
  the condemnor shall annually account for and remit the attributable
  revenue in proportion to the real property interests acquired from
  the affected former owners and successors.
         (b)  Revenue from a specific parcel must first be attributed
  to that parcel. Revenue inseparable among several condemned
  parcels shall be allocated using independently appraised
  contribution to the revenue-generating use, or acreage if
  contribution cannot reasonably be distinguished. No contractual
  affiliate, successor, governmental corporation, or project sponsor
  may evade payment through internal transfers or noncash
  consideration.
         (c)  Public taxes and general utility tariffs collected for
  services that cannot reasonably be attributed to the affected
  parcel are not deemed property-generated receipts merely because a
  public facility crosses the parcel. This narrow identification
  rule does not exempt site-specific commercial revenue and shall be
  reviewed by legislative counsel for conformity with the proposed
  constitutional amendment and adopted legislative priority.
         (d)  The condemnor shall maintain an audited annual
  property-revenue ledger and send statements and distributions to
  known former owners. An affected owner may audit the records, seek
  an accounting and injunction, and recover wrongfully withheld
  amounts, interest, and reasonable fees. A compensation award under
  Section 2206.210 may not be used as an offset to revenue due under
  this section.
         Sec. 2206.213.  TRANSPARENCY; OVERSIGHT; ENFORCEMENT. (a)
  The comptroller shall maintain a free statewide registry of covered
  projects and the condemning authority; requested parcels and
  acreage; number of owners; purpose and underlying legal authority;
  assessments and alternatives; panel reports; elections; court
  rulings; use-commencement dates; disposition, repurchase, and
  revenue remittances; and compliance and enforcement actions.
         (b)  The attorney general may seek injunctive or declaratory
  relief to prevent an unauthorized taking. An affected owner may
  bring an action for declaratory or injunctive relief and
  enforcement of a mandatory duty in a district court of the county
  where the land lies. Jurisdiction and a limited waiver of
  governmental and sovereign immunity to suit and liability are
  provided to the extent necessary for the prospective relief and
  specific fee and repurchase remedies expressly conferred by this
  subchapter; this subsection creates no general tort damages remedy.
         (c)  Materially false verification, evasion by project
  segmentation, concealment of beneficial ownership, or retaliation
  for participation disqualifies the defective application until
  corrected and is subject to court-ordered injunctive relief.
  Additional criminal or civil penalties require independent
  Legislative Council review before inclusion.
         Sec. 2206.214.  NARROW TEMPORARY LIFE-SAFETY RELIEF. A
  district court may authorize only temporary, reasonably necessary
  access or a nonpermanent property interest for a documented
  imminent threat to human life when no feasible response exists
  without immediate access. The order must specify maximum duration,
  safeguards, payment, restoration, and a hearing within 10 days. It
  does not authorize a permanent taking, ordinary growth project,
  speculative capacity expansion, or evasion of the necessity
  requirement.
         Sec. 2206.215.  PROTECTED RIGHTS; FEDERAL LAW. (a) This
  subchapter applies prospectively to petitions filed on or after its
  effective date, subject to the constitutional protection of final
  judgments, interests already acquired, and legally protected
  contracts. A pending route designation, project plan, preliminary
  expenditure, or unfiled proposal alone is not a vested condemnation
  of a particular parcel.
         (b)  This subchapter does not authorize the state to regulate
  direct federal condemnation or diminish rights under the
  Constitution of the United States. Any federally preempted
  application is severed only to the minimum degree required by
  controlling law; the remaining state-law duties continue where
  lawful.
         Sec. 2206.216.  RULES; NO WAIVER. The comptroller, the
  attorney general, the Texas Department of Transportation, the
  Public Utility Commission, the Railroad Commission, and the Texas
  Commission on Environmental Quality may coordinate published
  implementation guidance within their lawful jurisdictions, but may
  not create exceptions or substitute implied approval for a
  statutory or constitutional protection.
         SECTION 4.  Section 2206.001, Government Code, is amended by
  adding Subsection (f) to read as follows:
         (f)  An exception recognized in Subsection (c) preserves
  only the underlying authority otherwise granted by law; it does not
  exempt a condemnor from the public necessity, election, independent
  review, compensation, and other requirements of Subchapter E,
  subject to controlling federal law.
         SECTION 5.  Section 2206.053, Government Code, is amended by
  adding Subsection (h) to read as follows:
         (h)  Notwithstanding Subsection (f) or (g), an ordinance,
  resolution, or delegation that does not identify a parcel or that
  authorizes condemnation without compliance with Subchapter E may
  not substitute for the parcel-specific public necessity findings,
  independent review, or county election required by that subchapter.
         SECTION 6.  Chapter 21, Property Code, is amended by adding
  Section 21.0125 to read as follows:
         Sec. 21.0125.  PRIOR PUBLIC NECESSITY DETERMINATION. (a)
  Notwithstanding Sections 21.001, 21.011, 21.014, and 21.021,
  before special commissioners are appointed or possession granted,
  the district court in the county containing the property shall make
  the findings required by Subchapter E, Chapter 2206, Government
  Code, when applicable.
         (b)  No compensation award, deposit, temporary injunction
  bond, or waiver of commissioners' objections dispenses with the
  prior judicial determination and any timely interlocutory appeal.
  This section supplements Section 21.003.
         SECTION 7.  Chapter 21, Property Code, is amended by adding
  Section 21.0431 to read as follows:
         Sec. 21.0431.  MINIMUM COMPENSATION FOR CERTAIN TAKINGS.
  For a taking covered by Subchapter E, Chapter 2206, Government
  Code, special commissioners and courts shall award no less than the
  compensation required by Section 2206.210 of that code. A written
  settlement made with independently represented owners may
  establish different payment terms only if it does not purport to
  waive any required constitutional minimum or entitlement of another
  owner.
         SECTION 8.  Section 21.101, Property Code, is amended by
  adding Subsection (d) to read as follows:
         (d)  For property acquired under Subchapter E, Chapter 2206,
  Government Code, the additional triggers, ten-year actual-use
  test, notice protections, and remedies in Section 2206.211,
  Government Code, apply in addition to this subchapter; an existing
  stronger repurchase right is preserved.
         SECTION 9.  Section 21.103, Property Code, is amended by
  adding Subsection (c) to read as follows:
         (c)  The repurchase price for a property interest acquired
  under Subchapter E, Chapter 2206, Government Code, is governed by
  Section 2206.211, Government Code, rather than the unadjusted
  resale price in Subsection (b), without limiting another repurchase
  entitlement available for acquisitions not covered by that
  subchapter.
         SECTION 10.  CONFORMING EFFECT ON OTHER CODES. The
  requirements of Subchapter E, Chapter 2206, Government Code, and
  Section 21.0125, Property Code, apply notwithstanding a
  conflicting condemnation grant or procedure in Transportation Code
  Chapter 203; Utilities Code Chapter 181 or 186; Water Code Chapter
  49; Natural Resources Code Chapter 111; Local Government Code
  Chapter 251; Education Code Section 11.155; or a special enabling
  law, subject to the supremacy of controlling federal law. An
  authorizing law that conflicts with this Act shall be read together
  with this Act to give effect to the stronger lawful protection
  against an involuntary taking.
         SECTION 11.  IMPLEMENTATION; LEGISLATIVE REVIEW. (a)
  Within 180 days after this Act takes effect, the comptroller shall
  publish a consolidated list of existing state-law entities claiming
  condemnation authority and the statutes that confer it, flag
  private or unelected bodies, and submit to the legislature proposed
  conforming repeals or limitations.
         (b)  No new prohibited taking may proceed solely because
  implementing rules have not yet been adopted. This Act does not
  appropriate funds. The fiscal effect of panels, elections,
  enhanced compensation, reporting, and revenue remittance must be
  addressed through the legislative appropriations process.
         SECTION 12.  TRANSITION. This Act governs condemnation
  petitions filed, and new acquisitions by express threat of
  condemnation initiated, on or after the effective date. It does not
  reopen final judgments or undo an interest actually and lawfully
  acquired before that date. A preexisting permit, certificate,
  plan, or contract does not by itself grandfather a future unfiled
  condemnation proceeding. Any application to a legally protected
  preexisting right shall be resolved under the Texas and United
  States Constitutions.
         SECTION 13.  SEVERABILITY. If a provision or application of
  this Act is held invalid, remaining provisions capable of
  independent application remain effective. A finding invalidating a
  referendum procedure, reimbursement formula, or delegation limit
  does not by itself authorize a taking prohibited by another
  effective provision.
         SECTION 14.  EFFECTIVE DATE AND CONTINGENCY. This Act takes
  effect January 1, 2028, but only if the constitutional amendment
  proposed by the 90th Legislature, Regular Session, 2027, concerning
  the public-necessity standard for eminent domain is approved by the
  voters at the November 2, 2027 election. If that amendment is not
  approved, this Act has no effect.
