By: Zaffirini S.B. No. 34
 
 
 
   
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to county authority to regulate the development of certain
  large electricity users.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1   Chapter 231, Local Government Code, is amended
  by adding Subchapter N to read as follows:
  SUBCHAPTER N.  COUNTY REGULATION OF LARGE-LOAD ELECTRICITY USERS
         Sec. 231.301.  DEFINITIONS.  In this subchapter:
               (1)  "Large computational load facility" means a tract
  or one or more contiguous or noncontiguous tracts of land under
  common ownership or control that are developed or proposed for
  development for use by a large computational load.
               (2)  "County large computational load zoning district"
  means a zoning district established under this subchapter.
               (3)  "Large computational load" means a business that:
                     (A)  is primarily engaged in:
                           (i)  creating, executing, and delivering
  software applications and services;
                           (ii)  artificial intelligence training,
  deployment, and delivery;
                           (iii)  adding virtual currency transactions
  to a distributed ledger; or
                           (iv)  other computational activities at a
  data center; and
                     (B)  is a large load under Section 37.0561,
  Utilities Code; or
                     (C)  a substation, transmission or distribution
  facilities, or other electric infrastructure designed to serve the
  facility or project; and
                     (D)  in determining whether a facility or project
  meets the requirements of Paragraph (B), the county may aggregate
  related facilities, phases, contiguous or noncontiguous tracts,
  meters, or projects that are under common ownership or control or
  are developed, financed, constructed, or operated as part of a
  common plan.
               (4)  "Project" includes the construction of a new large
  computational load or a material expansion, redevelopment, or
  change in the use of an existing facility. A material expansion is
  an increase in electricity demand of at least 10 megawatts.
         Sec. 231.302. APPLICABILITY. This subchapter applies only to
  the unincorporated area of a county.
         Sec. 231.303.  AUTHORITY TO ESTABLISH LARGE COMPUTATIONAL
  LOAD ZONING DISTRICTS.  (a)  To protect the public health, safety,
  and welfare, promote the orderly development of the county, and
  ensure that county residents benefit from large computational load
  development, the commissioners court may establish one or more
  county large computational load zoning districts and regulate the
  location and development of large computational load and large
  computational load within a district consistent with this
  subchapter.
         (b)  A district may include all or part of the unincorporated
  area of the county.  The boundaries of the district must be
  described by metes and bounds or shown on a map adopted as part of
  the order establishing the district.
         (c)  A zoning district or district boundary is not effective
  until adopted by the commissioners court after a public hearing.
  Before adopting a district or district boundary, the commissioners
  court shall provide public notice and hold a public hearing in
  accordance with procedures adopted by the court.
         (d)  After the commissioners court provides notice of a
  hearing to establish or amend a county large computational load
  zoning district, a person may not begin construction or development
  of, or materially expand, redevelop, or materially change the use
  of, a county large computational load facility within the area
  described in the notice until the commissioners court:
               (1)  adopts or rejects the proposed district or
  amendment; or
               (2)  fails to take action on the proposed district or
  amendment before the 91st day after the date notice is provided.
         Sec. 231.304. ZONING REGULATIONS. (a)  The commissioners
  court may adopt reasonable zoning regulations within a county large
  computational load zoning district governing county large
  computational load facilities and large computational load,
  including regulations addressing:
               (1)  the location of a county large computational load
  facility;
               (2)  minimum lot size and tract dimensions;
               (3)  setbacks, buffers, landscaping, screening, and
  open-space requirements;
               (4)  measures reasonably necessary to protect nearby
  residential, educational, medical, agricultural, or
  environmentally sensitive uses from incompatible development;
               (5)  access roads, traffic mitigation, emergency
  access, and transportation impacts;
               (6)  noise, vibration, exterior lighting, and other
  off-site impacts;
               (7)  water supply, water conservation, cooling
  technology, wastewater service, stormwater management, drainage,
  floodplain management, and land subsidence risks, as applicable;
               (8)  fire protection, emergency response, law
  enforcement, and other public safety considerations;
               (9)  improvements, mitigation measures, capacity
  requirements, and service arrangements necessary to address
  project-related demands on county roads, bridges, drainage and
  flood-mitigation infrastructure, facilities, services, and county
  infrastructure capacity;
               (10)  disclosure of information necessary for the
  county to evaluate impacts on public infrastructure, water
  resources, emergency services, public safety, and county finances,
  subject to Chapter 552, Government Code;
               (11)  reasonable mitigation measures, road-use
  agreements, infrastructure agreements, and cost-sharing agreements
  reasonably related to project impacts and necessary to ensure that
  project-related county infrastructure, mitigation, remediation,
  and service costs are borne by the project rather than county
  taxpayers;
               (12)  performance bonds, closure bonds, site-
  restoration commitments, and other financial assurances necessary
  to ensure compliance and address abandonment, prolonged
  inactivity, decommissioning, demolition, remediation, removal of
  equipment or hazardous materials, and final site restoration; and
               (13)  any other reasonable land-use regulation
  directly related to the public health, safety, or welfare or the
  orderly development of the county.
         (b)  A zoning regulation adopted under this section must be
  reasonably related to a documented or reasonably foreseeable impact
  of a large computational load and may not be arbitrary or unrelated
  to a purpose authorized by this subchapter.
         (c)  The county may determine the amount, form, duration,
  issuer, renewal requirements, and conditions for release of a
  performance bond, closure bond, letter of credit, or other
  financial assurance.  The amount must be reasonably related to the
  anticipated cost of compliance, mitigation, remediation,
  decommissioning, or site restoration.
         (d)  A county may not adopt regulations that directly
  conflict with a regulation adopted by a state agency.
         Sec. 231.305.  SPECIAL USE PERMIT. (a) The commissioners
  court may require a person to obtain a special use permit before
  developing, constructing, materially expanding, redeveloping, or
  materially changing a county large computational load facility
  within a county large computational load zoning district.
         (b)  An application for a special use permit may require:
               (1)  submission of site plans and engineering studies;
               (2) information regarding traffic, water supply,
  wastewater, drainage, floodplain, emergency service, public
  safety, and county infrastructure impact analyses;
               (3)  information regarding anticipated electric
  demand, energization schedules, load growth, and electric
  infrastructure associated with the project;
               (4)  identification of any proposed water source and
  information regarding the availability of an adequate long-term
  water supply;
               (5)  supporting documentation prepared or certified by
  a professional engineer licensed in this state demonstrating that
  the proposed electric, water, wastewater, and other utility service
  and any required infrastructure improvements are reasonably
  capable of serving the projected demand of the project without
  impairing existing service;
               (6)  documentation that the project has been submitted
  to any applicable review or interconnection process required by the
  Public Utility Commission of Texas or administered by the Electric
  Reliability Council of Texas or another entity with jurisdiction;
               (7)  information reasonably necessary to identify and
  evaluate measures to mitigate documented adverse impacts within the
  county's authority under this subchapter.
         (c)  Before acting on a special use permit, the commissioners
  court may provide public notice and may hold a public hearing in
  accordance with procedures adopted by the court.
         (d)  The county may determine whether an application is
  complete and may require the applicant to submit additional
  information reasonably necessary to evaluate the application.  A
  permit application is not considered filed until the county
  determines that the application is complete.
         (e)  The commissioners court may approve, conditionally
  approve, or deny a special use permit based on compliance with this
  subchapter and zoning regulations adopted under this subchapter.  
  The county may make the final decision and any conditions imposed on
  the permit available to the public in a manner prescribed by the
  commissioners court.
         (f)  A permit may be denied if the applicant fails to
  demonstrate compliance with applicable zoning regulations or fails
  to adequately address a documented or reasonably foreseeable impact
  within the county's authority under this subchapter.
         (g)  After notice and an opportunity to cure, the county may
  suspend or revoke a special use permit for a material violation of
  this subchapter, a zoning regulation, or a condition of the permit.
         (h)  The county may issue a stop-work order if construction
  or development is occurring without a required permit or in
  material violation of a zoning regulation or permit condition. The
  order remains effective until the violation is corrected or the
  order is modified or withdrawn by the county or a court.
         (i)  The commissioners court may impose reasonable
  application, inspection, administrative, and professional-review
  fees including the reasonable cost of qualified consultants
  retained by the county, in an amount not to exceed the county's
  actual costs of administering this subchapter and reviewing the
  application.
         (j)  Information submitted under this section is subject to
  Chapter 552, Government Code. A county may not enter into an
  agreement requiring the county to withhold information from public
  disclosure except to the extent withholding is authorized by that
  chapter or other law.
         Sec. 231.306.  ELECTRIC REGULATION; PROJECT INFORMATION.  
  (a)  This subchapter does not authorize a county to regulate:
               (l)  electric rates or electric service;
               (1-a) water rates or water service;
               (2)  generation permitting;
               (3)  transmission or distribution facility siting or
  interconnection standards; and
               (4)  the operations of the Public Utility Commission of
  Texas or the Electric Reliability Council of Texas;
         (b)  In evaluating a project under this subchapter, a county
  may request and consider information regarding:
               (1)  anticipated electric demand;
               (2)  the timing and scale of load growth;
               (3)  planned substations, transmission or distribution
  facilities, or other electric infrastructure associated with the
  project;
               (4)  the status of the project in any applicable review
  or interconnection process required or administered by the Public
  Utility Commission of Texas or the Electric Reliability Council of
  Texas; and
               (5)  measures proposed to reduce impacts on county
  infrastructure, emergency services, public safety, water supplies,
  and neighboring property.
         (c)  Nothing in this section prohibits a county from
  requesting or considering information concerning electric demand,
  energization schedules, substations, transmission or distribution
  infrastructure, interconnection status, reliability impacts, or
  curtailment capability for the purpose of evaluating land use,
  roads, drainage, water, emergency services, public safety,
  neighboring property, or county financial impacts.
         Sec. 231.307.  NONCONFORMING USES. (a) A county large
  computational load facility lawfully developed, or a large
  computational load lawfully operating before the effective date of
  a zoning regulation adopted under this subchapter may continue as a
  lawful nonconforming use.
         (b)  Zoning regulations adopted under this subchapter apply
  to a material expansion, redevelopment, or change in use of a
  nonconforming county large computational load facility or large
  computational load. In evaluating an expansion, redevelopment, or
  change in use, the county may consider the cumulative impacts of the
  existing county large computational load facility or large
  computational load and the proposed activity.
         (c)  A county large computational load facility or large
  computational load is not entitled to nonconforming-use status
  solely because land has been purchased, a project has been
  announced, utility service has been requested, or a preliminary
  application has been submitted.
         Sec. 231.308.  ENFORCEMENT.  (a)  A county may bring an
  action for injunctive relief to prevent or restrain a violation of
  this subchapter, a zoning regulation adopted under this subchapter,
  or a special use permit issued under this subchapter.
         (b)  A person who violates this subchapter, a zoning
  regulation adopted under this subchapter, or a special use permit
  issued under this subchapter is liable to the county for a civil
  penalty of not less than $500 or more than $10,000 for each
  violation for each day the violation continues.
         (c)  The county attorney or district attorney may bring an
  action under this section in a district court in the county.
         (d)  A county that prevails in an enforcement action under
  this section may recover reasonable attorney's fees, court costs,
  investigative costs, and expert-witness fees.
         Sec. 231.309.  CONSTRUCTION OF SUBCHAPTER. (a) This
  subchapter shall be liberally construed to accomplish its purposes
  of protecting the public health, safety, and welfare; promoting the
  orderly development of each county; and protecting neighboring
  property and public infrastructure;
         (b)  This subchapter may not be construed to authorize a
  county to regulate electric rates, electric service, wholesale
  electric markets, electric reliability standards, or a matter
  assigned by state or federal law to the Public Utility Commission of
  Texas, the Electric Reliability Council of Texas, or a federal
  agency.
         Sec. 231.310.  COORDINATION WITH COUNTY SUBDIVISION
  REGULATION.  (a)  Notwithstanding Section 232.001(h), a county may
  require an applicant for a plat or replat of property located within
  a large computational load zoning district to submit information,
  analyses, studies, plans, agreements, and financial assurances
  authorized by this subchapter or regulations adopted under this
  subchapter.
         (b)  Notwithstanding Section 232.0025, an application for a
  plat or replat of property located within a county large
  computational load zoning district is not considered complete, and
  the period within which the commissioners court or county authority
  must approve, conditionally approve, or disapprove the application
  does not begin, until:
               (1)  the county determines that the application
  includes the information required under this subchapter,
  regulations adopted under this subchapter, and Chapter 232; and
               (2)  any special use permit required for the
  development proposed in the application has been finally approved;
         (c)  The denial of a special use permit required for
  development proposed in a plat or replat application constitutes
  grounds for disapproval of the plat or replat application.
         SECTION 2.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
