By: Perez of El Paso H.B. No. 878
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the acquisition of single-family homes in this state by
  corporate owners for use as rental property, the attribution of
  ownership among persons under common control, and the registration
  of those owners; providing a civil penalty.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Chapter 5, Property Code, is amended by adding
  Subchapter I to read as follows:
  SUBCHAPTER I. ACQUISITION OF SINGLE-FAMILY HOMES BY CORPORATE
  OWNERS
         Sec. 5.301.  DEFINITIONS. In this subchapter: 
               (1)  "Beneficial owner" means a person that, directly
  or indirectly, through any contract, arrangement, understanding,
  relationship, or otherwise, holds or shares voting power,
  investment power, or an equity, membership, or profits interest in
  another person. 
               (2)  "Corporate owner" means a corporation,
  partnership, limited partnership, limited liability company,
  protected series or registered series of a limited liability
  company, business trust, land trust, real estate investment trust,
  joint venture, joint stock company, investment asset manager, bank,
  or other entity or arrangement, whether organized under the laws of
  this state, another state, or a foreign jurisdiction. The term does
  not include an individual.
               (3)  "Hold," with respect to a single-family home,
  means to own or otherwise have an interest in the home. A person
  does not hold a home solely by reason of an option or right of first
  refusal that has not been exercised.
               (4)  "Interest," with respect to a single-family home,
  includes a fee simple interest, a leasehold estate, a beneficial
  interest in a trust, an option or right of first refusal, and a
  contractual right to receive substantially all of the economic
  benefit of the home.
               (5)  "Rental property" means a single-family home that
  is used, offered, or held out for use as a residence by a person
  other than the owner in exchange for compensation, including use
  for a term of less than 30 days. 
               (6)  "Single-family home" means a structure located in
  this state that is designed for occupancy as a residence by one
  family, including a condominium unit or townhome, together with the
  lot or parcel on which the structure is located. The term does not
  include a manufactured home or a mobile home.
         Sec. 5.302.  ATTRIBUTION OF OWNERSHIP; COMMON CONTROL. (a)
  For purposes of this subchapter, a single-family home held by a
  person is considered to be held by each other person with which that
  person is under common control, and all persons under common
  control are considered a single corporate owner. 
         (b)  Common control exists if, directly or indirectly,
  including through one or more intermediaries: 
               (1)  a person owns, controls, holds with power to vote,
  or holds a beneficial interest in 25 percent or more of the equity,
  membership, profits, or voting interests of another person; 
               (2)  the same person, or a group of persons acting in
  concert, holds an interest described by Subdivision (1) in two or
  more persons; 
               (3)  a majority of the officers, directors, managers,
  managing members, general partners, or trustees of one person also
  serve in one of those capacities for another person; 
               (4)  a person serves as the investment adviser, asset
  manager, or general partner of another person, or two or more
  persons are advised or managed by the same person or by affiliates
  of the same person, and the adviser, manager, or general partner has
  discretion to direct the acquisition or disposition of
  single-family homes; 
               (5)  a person has the right, by contract or otherwise,
  to direct or to approve the acquisition or disposition of
  single-family homes held by another person; or 
               (6)  two or more persons share a common ultimate
  beneficial owner.
         (c)  A protected series or registered series established
  under Subchapter M, Chapter 101, Business Organizations Code, the
  limited liability company that established the series, and each
  other series of that company are considered a single corporate
  owner. 
         (d)  A single-family home held by a nominee, agent, trustee,
  land trust, or other person for the benefit of a corporate owner is
  considered to be held by that corporate owner. 
         (e)  In determining whether persons are under common
  control, a court may consider whether two or more persons: 
               (1)  have one or more officers, directors, managers,
  managing members, general partners, or trustees in common; 
               (2)  engage the same person to manage single-family
  homes held by each of them; 
               (3)  share a principal place of business, officers, or
  employees; 
               (4)  share a common source of capital or obtain
  financing from a common lender under a common credit facility; 
               (5)  operate under a common trade name, brand, or
  marketing identity; 
               (6)  coordinate the acquisition, financing, leasing,
  or management of single-family homes; or 
               (7)  were organized by or at the direction of the same
  person.
         (f)  A transaction, entity, series, trust, or other
  arrangement a principal purpose of which is to avoid a limitation
  imposed by this subchapter is disregarded, and each single-family
  home held through the arrangement is considered to be held by the
  person for whose benefit the arrangement was created or is
  maintained. 
         (g)  An individual may be a person through which common
  control exists under Subsection (b). A single-family home held by
  an individual in the individual's own name is not attributed to a
  corporate owner under Subsection (a) and is not counted under
  Section 5.303.
         Sec. 5.303.  LIMITATION ON ACQUISITION. (a) A corporate
  owner that holds 10 or more single-family homes that are rental
  property may not: 
               (1)  acquire an interest in another single-family home;
  or
               (2)  enter into a contract or option to acquire an
  interest in another single-family home. 
         (b)  A corporate owner that holds fewer than 10 single-family
  homes that are rental property may not acquire an interest in a
  single-family home if the acquisition would cause the corporate
  owner to hold more than 10 single-family homes that are rental
  property. 
         (c)  A corporate owner that holds 10 or more single-family
  homes that are rental property may not use or offer for use as
  rental property an additional single-family home in which the
  corporate owner holds an interest.
         (d)  A corporate owner is not required to sell, divest, or
  otherwise dispose of a single-family home that the corporate owner
  held before September 1, 2027. A home held before that date is
  counted in determining the number of single-family homes a
  corporate owner holds for purposes of this section. 
         (e)  A contract entered into in violation of this section is
  voidable by the seller at any time before the contract is fully
  performed. A seller who avoids a contract under this subsection is
  entitled to recover court costs and reasonable attorney's fees. 
         (f)  This section applies without regard to whether the
  person from whom the interest is acquired is a corporate owner
  subject to this subchapter. 
         (g)  For purposes of this section, a person acquires an
  interest in a single-family home if the person acquires, directly
  or indirectly, an ownership interest in or control of a corporate
  owner that holds the home, including by merger, conversion,
  interest or share purchase, assignment, or any other transaction,
  and the acquisition results in the person and that corporate owner
  being under common control under Section 5.302. The number of
  single-family homes acquired is the number of such homes held by
  that corporate owner.
         (h)  This section does not apply to an acquisition of an
  interest in a single-family home from a person with which the
  acquiring person is under common control under Section 5.302 both
  immediately before and immediately after the acquisition, if the
  acquisition does not increase the total number of single-family
  homes that are rental property held by the persons under common
  control. This subsection applies to a transfer, merger,
  conversion, or other reorganization among persons under common
  control.
         Sec. 5.304.  EXEMPTIONS. (a) Section 5.303 does not apply
  to:
               (1)  this state, a political subdivision of this state,
  the United States, or an agency or instrumentality of any of them; 
               (2)  a qualified affordable housing organization, with
  respect to a single-family home that is subject to a restrictive
  covenant described by Subsection (c); 
               (3)  an entity that qualifies as a community home under
  Section 123.004, Human Resources Code, with respect to a home used
  as a community home;
               (4)  an employer that provides the home as a residence
  to an employee of the employer; 
               (5)  a person that acquires the home for the purpose of
  constructing or substantially rehabilitating a home for sale, for a
  period not to exceed five years after the date of acquisition, if
  the home is not used as rental property during that period; 
               (6)  a lender that acquires the home through
  foreclosure or a deed in lieu of foreclosure, for a period not to
  exceed three years after the date of acquisition; 
               (7)  a person that acquires the home by devise,
  descent, or distribution from a decedent's estate; or 
               (8)  a corporate owner, with respect to a single-family
  home that the corporate owner or a person under common control with
  the corporate owner constructed or caused to be constructed as
  rental property, if: 
                     (A)  the home has never been occupied as a
  residence by an owner of the home; 
                     (B)  the home has never been offered for sale to
  the public as an owner-occupied residence; and 
                     (C)  the corporate owner has held the home
  continuously since the date construction of the home was completed. 
         (b)  In this section, "qualified affordable housing
  organization" means:
               (1)  an organization that, for at least the three years
  preceding the date of the acquisition, has been exempt from federal
  income taxation under Section 501(a), Internal Revenue Code of
  1986, by being listed as an exempt organization under Section
  501(c)(3) of that code and has had as one of its purposes the
  provision of housing to individuals or families of low income, and a
  majority of the members of whose board of directors have their
  principal place of residence in this state; 
               (2)  a limited partnership of which an organization
  described by Subdivision (1) controls 100 percent of the general
  partner interest; or 
               (3)  an entity the parent of which is an organization
  described by Subdivision (1). 
               An entity described by Subdivision (2) or (3) must be
  organized under the laws of this state and have its principal place
  of business in this state.
         (c)  A restrictive covenant described by this subsection is a
  covenant recorded in the real property records of the county in
  which the single-family home is located that, for a term of not less
  than 30 years, requires that: 
               (1)  the home be rented only to an individual or family
  whose annual household income does not exceed 60 percent of the
  greater of the area median family income for the household's place
  of residence or the statewide area median family income, in each
  case as adjusted for family size and as established by the United
  States Department of Housing and Urban Development; 
               (2)  the annual total of the monthly rent charged for
  the home, plus the organization's reasonable estimate of the cost
  of utilities paid by the tenant other than telephone, cable, and
  Internet service, not exceed 30 percent of the income limit that
  applies to the home under Subdivision (1); and 
               (3)  the organization certify compliance with
  Subdivisions (1) and (2) annually to the comptroller in the
  registration required by Section 5.305. 
         (d)  A restrictive covenant described by Subsection (c) is
  enforceable by the attorney general, by the municipality or county
  in which the home is located, and by a tenant of the home, each of
  whom may bring an action for injunctive relief, for damages, or
  both, and is entitled to recover court costs and reasonable
  attorney's fees on prevailing. 
         (e)  A single-family home that is subject to a restrictive
  covenant described by Subsection (c) is not counted in determining
  the number of single-family homes a corporate owner holds for
  purposes of Section 5.303. If the covenant is released, expires, or
  is determined not to be enforceable, the home is counted from the
  date of that event.
         (f)  A corporate owner that charges rent in excess of the
  amount permitted by Subsection (c)(2), or that rents a home subject
  to a restrictive covenant described by Subsection (c) to a
  household whose income exceeds the limit under Subsection (c)(1),
  is liable to this state for a civil penalty in an amount not to
  exceed $25,000 for each home for each year in which the violation
  occurs, and the exemption under Subsection (a)(2) does not apply to
  that home.
         (g)  The exemption under Subsection (a)(8) applies only to
  the corporate owner described by that subdivision. The exemption
  does not apply to a person that acquires the home, or that acquires
  an ownership interest in or control of the corporate owner, and
  Section 5.303 applies to that acquisition. The exemption terminates
  on the date of the acquisition. 
         (h)  On the expiration of a period prescribed by Subsection
  (a)(5) or (a)(6), the single-family home is counted in determining
  the number of single-family homes the corporate owner holds for
  purposes of Section 5.303. This subsection does not require a
  corporate owner to sell, divest, or otherwise dispose of the home.
         Sec. 5.305.  REGISTRATION; DISCLOSURE OF BENEFICIAL
  OWNERSHIP. (a) Not later than March 1 of each year, a corporate
  owner that holds five or more single-family homes that are rental
  property shall register with the comptroller and report: 
               (1)  the corporate owner's name, organizational form,
  jurisdiction of organization, and registered agent in this state; 
               (2)  the address and the appraisal district account
  number of each single-family home the corporate owner holds that is
  rental property; 
               (3)  the name of each person with which the corporate
  owner is under common control under Section 5.302 and the number of
  single-family homes held by each; and
               (4)  the name and address of each beneficial owner that
  holds, directly or indirectly, 25 percent or more of the corporate
  owner. 
         (b)  A corporate owner shall supplement a registration not
  later than the 30th day after the date the corporate owner acquires
  or disposes of a single-family home that is rental property or the
  date information reported under Subsection (a)(3) or (4) changes. 
         (c)  The comptroller shall maintain a publicly accessible,
  searchable, and downloadable database of the information reported
  under this section and shall update the database at least monthly.
  The residence address of a beneficial owner who is an individual is
  excepted from required public disclosure under Chapter 552,
  Government Code; the name of the beneficial owner is not. 
         (d)  A corporate owner that fails to register or to
  supplement a registration as required by this section, or that
  makes a materially false or misleading statement in a registration,
  is liable to this state for a civil penalty in an amount not to
  exceed $10,000 for each single-family home that was not accurately
  reported for each year the home was not accurately reported. 
         (e)  The comptroller shall adopt rules necessary to
  administer this section, including rules prescribing the form and
  manner of registration and the manner in which the database is
  maintained. 
         Sec. 5.306.  CIVIL PENALTY; ENFORCEMENT. (a) A corporate
  owner that violates Section 5.303 is liable to this state for a
  civil penalty in an amount not to exceed $100,000 for each
  single-family home acquired, or used or offered for use as rental
  property, in violation of that section.
         (b)  In addition to the penalty under Subsection (a), a
  person is liable to this state for a civil penalty in an amount not
  to exceed $100,000 for each entity, series, trust, or other
  arrangement the person created, maintained, or used in a manner
  described by Section 5.302(f). 
         (c)  In determining the amount of a civil penalty under this
  subchapter, the court shall consider: 
               (1)  the seriousness of the violation and the number of
  single-family homes involved; 
               (2)  whether the violation was intentional or part of a
  pattern of violations; 
               (3)  the economic benefit the violator gained from the
  violation; 
               (4)  the violator's history of previous violations; and
               (5)  any effort by the violator to correct the
  violation. 
         (d)  The attorney general, a district attorney, a criminal
  district attorney, a county attorney, or a municipality or county
  in which a single-family home that is the subject of the violation
  is located may bring an action to recover a civil penalty under this
  subchapter, to obtain injunctive relief, or both. 
         (e)  A civil penalty recovered under this subchapter shall be
  deposited to the credit of the general revenue fund, except that if
  the action is brought by a municipality or county, or by a district
  attorney, criminal district attorney, or county attorney on behalf
  of a municipality or county, one-half of the amount recovered shall
  be paid to that municipality or county. 
         (f)  The attorney general or other prevailing plaintiff is
  entitled to recover reasonable attorney's fees, court costs, and
  investigative costs incurred in bringing an action under this
  subchapter.
         (g)  The remedies provided by this subchapter are in addition
  to any other remedy provided by law. 
         Sec. 5.307.  SEVERABILITY. If any provision of this
  subchapter or its application to any person or circumstance is held
  invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or
  applications of this subchapter that can be given effect without
  the invalid provision or application, and to this end the
  provisions of this subchapter are severable. 
         SECTION 2.  Subchapter C, Chapter 86, Education Code, is
  amended by adding Section 86.56 to read as follows:
         Sec. 86.56.  REPORT ON CORPORATE OWNERSHIP OF SINGLE-FAMILY
  HOMES. (a) In this section, "corporate owner," "rental property,"
  and "single-family home" have the meanings assigned by Section
  5.301, Property Code. 
         (b)  Not later than June 1 of each year, the center shall
  compile and publish a report on the acquisition, ownership, sale,
  and rental of single-family homes by corporate owners in this
  state, using the information reported under Section 5.305, Property
  Code, appraisal district records, and other available information.
  The comptroller shall provide the center the information reported
  under Section 5.305, Property Code. The report must include, for
  the state and for each county and metropolitan statistical area,
  the number of single-family homes held by corporate owners, the
  number acquired and sold during the preceding year, and the number
  held by corporate owners holding 10 or more such homes. 
         (c)  The center shall deliver the report to the governor, the
  lieutenant governor, the speaker of the house of representatives,
  and each member of the legislature, and shall publish the report on
  the center's Internet website.
         SECTION 3.  (a) Subchapter I, Chapter 5, Property Code, as
  added by this Act, applies only to an acquisition made on or after
  the effective date of this Act, other than an acquisition made under
  a contract or option entered into before that date, and to a
  contract or option entered into on or after the effective date of
  this Act.
         (b)  Section 5.303(c), Property Code, as added by this Act,
  applies only to the use or offering of a single-family home as
  rental property that begins on or after the effective date of this
  Act.
         (c)  The first registration required by Section 5.305,
  Property Code, as added by this Act, is due March 1, 2028. The
  comptroller shall adopt the rules required by Section 5.305(e),
  Property Code, as added by this Act, not later than January 1, 2028,
  and shall make the database required by Section 5.305(c), Property
  Code, as added by this Act, publicly available not later than June
  1, 2028.
         (d)  The first report required by Section 86.56, Education
  Code, as added by this Act, is due June 1, 2028.
         SECTION 4.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
