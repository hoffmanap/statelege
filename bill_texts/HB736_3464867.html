By: Perez of El Paso H.B. No. 736
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the liability of a person operating a detention
  facility in this state for the death of or injury to an individual
  in custody and for the failure to maintain and disclose custody
  records; providing a civil cause of action.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Subtitle C, Title 4, Civil Practice and Remedies
  Code, is amended by adding Chapter 156 to read as follows:
  CHAPTER 156. DETENTION FACILITY CUSTODY LIABILITY
         Sec. 156.001.  DEFINITIONS. In this chapter:
               (1)  "Child" means an individual younger than 18 years
  of age.
               (2)  "Custody" means the restriction of an individual's
  liberty by an operator at a detention facility, whether or not the
  restriction is imposed in connection with a criminal charge or
  conviction.
               (3)  "Detention facility" means a building or
  structure, or a portion of a building or structure, located in this
  state in which one or more individuals are held in custody,
  including a facility operated under a contract or other agreement
  with a governmental entity.
               (4)  "Operator" means a person that operates or manages
  a detention facility or that provides custodial, medical, mental
  health, or security services at a detention facility. The term does
  not include the United States, an agency of the United States, or an
  officer or employee of the United States acting within the scope of
  that person's employment.
               (5)  "Serious bodily injury" has the meaning assigned
  by Section 1.07, Penal Code.
         Sec. 156.002.  APPLICABILITY; CONSTRUCTION. (a) This
  chapter applies to a detention facility located in this state,
  including a detention facility located on land that is subject to
  the exclusive jurisdiction of the United States, to the extent
  permitted by 28 U.S.C. Section 5001 or other federal law.
         (b)  This chapter does not apply to the United States, an
  agency of the United States, or an officer or employee of the United
  States acting within the scope of that person's employment.
         (c)  This chapter does not create a cause of action against
  the United States and does not purport to waive the sovereign
  immunity of the United States.
         (d)  A duty imposed by this chapter is a duty of each person
  that operates a detention facility, without regard to whether the
  person is a governmental entity.
         Sec. 156.003.  DUTY OF CARE. An operator owes to each
  individual in custody at a detention facility operated by the
  operator a duty to exercise ordinary care to:
               (1)  provide necessary medical care, mental health
  care, and medication;
               (2)  provide adequate food, water, sanitation,
  hygiene, ventilation, heating, cooling, and shelter;
               (3)  protect the individual from violence, sexual
  abuse, and self-harm;
               (4)  supervise the individual and conduct welfare
  checks at reasonable intervals; and
               (5)  maintain conditions that do not present a
  substantial risk of serious harm to the individual's health or
  safety.
         Sec. 156.004.  LIABILITY FOR DEATH OR SERIOUS BODILY INJURY.
  (a) An operator is liable for damages for the death of or serious
  bodily injury to an individual in custody at a detention facility
  operated by the operator if the operator's breach of a duty under
  Section 156.003 was a proximate cause of the death or injury.
         (b)  A claimant who prevails in an action under this section
  may recover actual damages, including damages for mental anguish,
  and, on a finding of gross negligence, exemplary damages.
         Sec. 156.005.  CUSTODY RECORDS; CONTACT BETWEEN FAMILY
  MEMBERS. (a) An operator shall create and maintain, for each
  individual taken into custody at a detention facility operated by
  the operator, a record that includes:
               (1)  the individual's name and date of birth;
               (2)  the date, time, and place the individual was taken
  into custody at the facility;
               (3)  the name and, if known, the location of each adult
  and each child who was taken into custody with the individual or who
  was in the individual's care when the individual was taken into
  custody; and
               (4)  the date, time, and destination of each transfer
  of the individual from the facility.
         (b)  An operator may not transfer a child from a detention
  facility to a location outside this state unless, before the
  transfer, the operator records the identity and last known location
  of each adult who was taken into custody with the child and provides
  that information and notice of the transfer to the attorney general
  and, if known, to a parent or legal guardian of the child.
         (c)  On request, an operator shall disclose the information
  maintained under Subsection (a) to the individual who is the
  subject of the record, the individual's attorney, a parent or legal
  guardian of a child who is the subject of the record, or a court.
         (d)  An operator shall permit a child in custody at a
  detention facility to communicate, not less than once in each
  24-hour period, with each adult who was taken into custody with the
  child, unless a court has ordered otherwise.
         (e)  An operator shall retain a record required by this
  section for not less than seven years after the date the individual
  is released or transferred.
         Sec. 156.006.  LIABILITY FOR LOSS OF CONTACT BETWEEN FAMILY
  MEMBERS. An operator is liable for damages to an individual who is
  or was in custody at a detention facility operated by the operator,
  and to a child who was taken into custody with that individual, if
  the operator fails to comply with Section 156.005 and the failure
  was a proximate cause of the loss of contact between the individual
  and the child or of the inability of either to determine the other's
  location.
         Sec. 156.007.  CIVIL ACTION. (a) An action under this
  chapter may be brought by:
               (1)  an individual who is or was in custody at the
  detention facility;
               (2)  a parent, child, spouse, sibling, or legal
  guardian of an individual who is or was in custody at the detention
  facility; or
               (3)  the personal representative of the estate of a
  deceased individual who was in custody at the detention facility.
         (b)  In addition to damages recoverable under Section
  156.004, a claimant who prevails in an action for a violation of
  Section 156.005 is entitled to recover the greater of actual
  damages or $10,000 for each violation.
         (c)  A court shall award court costs and reasonable
  attorney's fees to a claimant who prevails in an action under this
  chapter.
         (d)  A court may grant injunctive relief to prevent or remedy
  a violation of this chapter.
         (e)  An action under this chapter must be brought not later
  than four years after the date the cause of action accrues.
         (f)  An action under this chapter may be brought in a
  district court in the county in which the detention facility is
  located or in which the claimant resides.
         Sec. 156.008.  DEFENSES; LIMITATIONS. (a) It is not a
  defense to an action under this chapter that the operator acted
  under a contract or other agreement with a governmental entity,
  except to the extent the operator establishes that the specific
  conduct that is the basis of the claim was directed by the United
  States under authority validly conferred and that the operator
  exercised no discretion with respect to that conduct.
         (b)  Compliance with a standard adopted by a governmental
  entity is not a defense to an action under this chapter if the
  operator knew or reasonably should have known that the standard was
  insufficient to satisfy a duty under Section 156.003.
         (c)  A provision of a contract, agreement, or other
  instrument that waives or limits liability under this chapter is
  void as against public policy.
         Sec. 156.009.  CUMULATIVE REMEDIES. The remedies provided
  by this chapter are in addition to any other remedy provided by law.
         Sec. 156.010.  SEVERABILITY. If any provision of this
  chapter or its application to any person or circumstance is held
  invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or
  applications of this chapter that can be given effect without the
  invalid provision or application, and to this end the provisions of
  this chapter are severable.
         SECTION 2.  Chapter 156, Civil Practice and Remedies Code,
  as added by this Act, applies only to a cause of action that accrues
  on or after the effective date of this Act.
         SECTION 3.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
