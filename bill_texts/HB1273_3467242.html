By: Castellano H.B. No. 1273
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the siting, construction, permitting, and operation of
  certain data centers; requiring local option elections; imposing a
  temporary moratorium; establishing an annual payments to county
  residents; repealing certain tax exemptions; providing civil
  penalties.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Subtitle B, Title 5, Health and Safety Code, is
  amended by adding Chapter 398 to read as follows:
  CHAPTER 398. DATA CENTER SITING, COMMUNITY CONSENT, AND PUBLIC
  BENEFIT
  SUBCHAPTER A.  GENERAL PROVISIONS
         Sec. 398.001.  DEFINITIONS. In this chapter:
               (1)  "Artificial intelligence compute occupant" means
  a person that owns, leases, controls, or uses computing equipment
  at a covered data center primarily to train, develop, fine-tune, or
  operate a generative or other machine-learning model for commercial
  consideration. The term includes a person receiving dedicated
  computing capacity through a contract with the owner or operator of
  the center but does not include a retail customer making ordinary,
  incidental use of an artificial intelligence service.
               (2)  "Commission" means the Texas Commission on
  Environmental Quality.
               (3)  "Covered data center" means one or more buildings,
  facilities, or installations under common ownership or control, or
  designed or operated as an integrated project, that contain or are
  proposed to contain computing equipment used for processing,
  storage, distribution of data, or artificial intelligence, and
  that:
                     (A)  have a designed aggregate maximum electric
  demand of 20 megawatts or more; or
                     (B)  contain or are proposed to contain 50,000
  square feet or more of space primarily devoted to the equipment
  described by this subdivision.
               (4)  "Developer" includes an owner, operator,
  applicant, affiliate, successor, or person acting in concert to
  construct, expand, occupy, or operate a covered data center.
               (5)  "Local approval" means voter approval under
  Subchapter C.
               (6)  "Project" includes a new covered data center or
  expansion that increases a center's maximum electric demand by 10
  megawatts or more or gross equipment floor area by 25,000 square
  feet or more.
               (7)  "Host county" means the county in which a covered
  data center is located. If a covered data center occupies land in
  more than one county, each county is a host county and payments
  under Subchapter E must be allocated among the host counties in
  proportion to the maximum electric demand of the equipment located
  in each county.
               (8)  "Eligible resident" means a natural person whose
  principal residence is in a host county for at least 183 days of the
  calendar year for which a stipend is paid. The term includes a minor
  child; a payment to a minor shall be made to the child's parent or
  legal guardian on behalf of the child. Ownership of real property,
  citizenship status, and income may not be used as conditions of
  eligibility.
         Sec. 398.002.  AGGREGATION; EVASION PROHIBITED. (a) For
  purposes of this chapter, adjacent or nearby facilities within two
  miles that share a common developer, financing, utility
  interconnection, water supply, or operational plan are presumed to
  be one covered data center.
         (b)  A person may not divide, phase, lease, convey, or
  separately permit a project for the purpose of avoiding this
  chapter. The commission shall consider reasonably foreseeable
  future phases in determining applicability.
         Sec. 398.003.  APPLICABILITY; OTHER LAW. (a) This chapter
  applies to projects proposed after the effective date of the Act
  adding this chapter and to covered projects that have not commenced
  substantial physical construction on that date.
         (b)  This chapter does not dispense with any permit,
  approval, or requirement imposed by federal or state law, a
  groundwater conservation district, or a political subdivision.
         (c)  A permit, interconnection agreement, zoning approval,
  or incentive agreement does not substitute for approval required by
  this chapter.
  SUBCHAPTER B. TEMPORARY MORATORIUM
         Sec. 398.051.  TWO-YEAR MORATORIUM. (a) Before the second
  anniversary of the effective date of the Act adding this chapter, a
  state agency or political subdivision may not issue a permit,
  approval, authorization, or certificate for the construction or
  expansion of a project, and a utility may not energize a new or
  expanded electric service connection for a project.
         (b)  A person may not commence or continue substantial
  physical construction of a project during the period prescribed by
  Subsection (a), except work necessary to address an imminent threat
  to life, health, or the environment or to maintain a facility in
  lawful operation on the effective date of the Act adding this
  chapter.
         (c)  An application submitted during the moratorium may be
  received and reviewed, but no approval may be issued and no vested
  right to approval arises from its submission or review.
         Sec. 398.052.  EXISTING OPERATIONS. This subchapter does
  not prohibit continued operation at the lawfully authorized demand
  and floor area of a covered data center in commercial operation on
  the effective date of the Act adding this chapter.  An expansion
  remains subject to Section 398.051.
  SUBCHAPTER C. LOCAL OPTION ELECTION
         Sec. 398.101.  LOCAL ELECTION REQUIRED. (a) The commission
  may not issue a permit under this chapter unless the development of
  the project has been approved by a majority of voters voting at a
  local option election held under this subchapter.
         (b)  For a project in an unincorporated area, the county
  shall conduct the election among the registered voters of the
  county.  For a project within a municipality, the municipality
  shall conduct an election among its registered voters and the
  county shall conduct a separate election among its registered
  voters; both elections must approve the project.
         (c)  If the project crosses municipal or county boundaries,
  each affected municipality and county must approve the project by
  separate election.
         Sec. 398.102.  PETITION AND ORDER. (a) A developer seeking
  local approval shall file a request with the governing body of each
  political subdivision required to hold an election, together with a
  project description, projected peak demand, annual water use,
  emergency generation capacity, parcel description, and the
  disclosure required by Section 398.151.
         (b)  The governing body shall order an election for the next
  uniform election date under Section 41.001, Election Code, for
  which the election can lawfully be ordered and administered.  The
  developer shall reimburse each political subdivision for the
  reasonable cost of the election.
         Sec. 398.103.  BALLOT. The ballot shall be prepared to
  permit voting for or against the proposition:  "The approval of a
  [name of project] data center at [general location], with a
  proposed maximum electric demand of [number] megawatts and
  projected annual water use of [number] gallons, subject to state
  permitting, public disclosure, and annual payments to county
  residents."
         Sec. 398.104.  EFFECT OF REJECTION; MATERIAL CHANGE. (a) A
  project rejected at an election may not be submitted for another
  election before the second anniversary of that election.
         (b)  An approval does not authorize a material increase of
  more than 10 percent in the demand, water use, or physical footprint
  disclosed to voters.  A material increase requires another
  election.
         Sec. 398.105.  PUBLIC NOTICE. At least 60 days before the
  election, the developer shall provide a plain-language impact
  statement on a publicly accessible website and by first-class mail
  to residents of the host county.  The statement must identify
  projected electric and water demand, planned sources of electricity
  and water, potential noise, emissions, requested incentives,
  expected infrastructure costs, and the proposed annual county
  resident stipend and the method for calculating it.
  SUBCHAPTER D. STATE DATA CENTER PERMIT
         Sec. 398.151.  PERMIT REQUIRED. (a) A person may not
  construct, expand, or commence operation of a project without a
  data center impact permit issued by the commission in addition to
  all other approvals required by law.
         (b)  An application must contain independently verified
  studies of:
               (1)  projected hourly peak and annual electric demand,
  required transmission and distribution facilities, and effects on
  residential rates and grid reliability;
               (2)  daily and annual water withdrawals and consumptive
  use, drought-stage operation, aquifer and surface-water impacts,
  and effects on drinking-water availability;
               (3)  air emissions from emergency generation and other
  sources;
               (4)  noise at the nearest residence and property
  boundary, including low-frequency noise;
               (5)  cumulative impacts of other existing or proposed
  facilities in the same watershed or electric service territory; and
               (6)  site restoration, emergency response, financial
  assurance, and mitigation of adverse effects on surrounding
  communities.
         Sec. 398.152.  INTERAGENCY REVIEW; FINDINGS. (a) Before
  issuing a permit, the commission shall obtain written findings from
  the Public Utility Commission of Texas concerning electric
  reliability, system costs, and compliance with Section 37.0561,
  Utilities Code, and from the Texas Water Development Board
  concerning water supply and drought impacts.
         (b)  The commission may issue a permit only if the applicant
  establishes by a preponderance of the evidence that:
               (1)  the project will not materially impair the ability
  of local residents to obtain affordable, reliable electricity or
  drinking water;
               (2)  the developer will bear all reasonably
  attributable incremental interconnection and utility
  infrastructure costs, without shifting those costs to residential
  ratepayers;
               (3)  the project incorporates water-efficient cooling
  technology and an enforceable drought curtailment plan;
               (4)  noise, air emissions, and other nuisance impacts
  are minimized through best available practicable mitigation;
               (5)  required financial assurance and reclamation
  plans are sufficient; and
               (6)  the applicant has obtained local approval and
  entered into all agreements required by Subchapter E.
         Sec. 398.153.  PUBLIC CONTESTED CASE HEARING. (a) The
  commission shall provide notice of a complete application to
  residents of the host county and to each affected municipality,
  county, utility, and groundwater conservation district.
         (b)  On the request of an affected person, the commission
  shall refer the application to the State Office of Administrative
  Hearings for a contested case hearing.  A resident, property owner,
  or political subdivision within the host county is an affected
  person for purposes of this section.
         (c)  The applicant has the burden of proof.  The commission
  may not issue a permit before findings from the hearing are
  considered.
         Sec. 398.154.  MONITORING; REVIEW. (a) A permit holder
  shall meter electric consumption and water withdrawals and
  consumption separately for each project and submit quarterly
  verified reports to the commission, the Public Utility Commission
  of Texas, and the Texas Water Development Board.
         (b)  Reports, permit applications, compliance records, and
  beneficial ownership of a permit holder are public information,
  except for specific technical cybersecurity information protected
  by other law.
         (c)  The commission shall review a permit at least once every
  five years and may amend, suspend, or revoke the permit for material
  noncompliance.  A material increase in demand, consumption, or
  footprint is a new project subject to Subchapter C.
         Sec. 398.155.  FINANCIAL ASSURANCE. Before construction,
  the developer shall provide a bond or other financial assurance in
  an amount determined by the commission sufficient to fund site
  reclamation, groundwater remediation if applicable, and the
  correction of reasonably foreseeable off-site harms.
  SUBCHAPTER E. ANNUAL STIPEND FOR COUNTY RESIDENTS
         Sec. 398.201.  COUNTY RESIDENT STIPEND FUND. (a) A
  developer shall, as a condition of receiving and maintaining a
  permit under this chapter, establish an independently audited
  county resident stipend fund for each host county.  The fund shall
  be held in trust solely for payments to eligible residents of that
  county under this subchapter.
         (b)  A fund may not be used for general county operations,
  economic development subsidies, tax abatements, or payments to the
  developer or an affiliate.  The commission shall provide for
  independent administration, annual public audits, and disclosure
  of the amount received and the amount distributed.
         Sec. 398.202.  ANNUAL PAYMENT BY DATA CENTER. (a) For each
  calendar year during which a covered data center operates, the
  developer and operator are jointly and severally liable for an
  annual county resident stipend payment in an amount equal to the
  greater of:
               (1)  two percent of gross receipts derived from the
  provision of data processing, computing capacity, artificial
  intelligence training, inference, data storage, or related
  services performed using the covered data center; or
               (2)  $10,000 multiplied by the number of megawatts of
  the covered data center's maximum authorized electric demand.
         (b)  Gross receipts include amounts received by an affiliate
  or person under common control for services attributable to
  computing equipment at the covered data center.  For computing
  capacity used by the developer or an affiliate rather than sold to
  an unrelated customer, gross receipts shall include the fair market
  value of that computing capacity as determined under rules adopted
  by the commission.
         (c)  The payment is due March 1 for the preceding calendar
  year.  A partial year of operation is prorated by the number of days
  the center operated.  The obligation may not be avoided by a lease,
  a service agreement, division of a project, or transfer of the
  center to an affiliate.
         (d)  The developer shall file an annual sworn statement of
  gross receipts and authorized demand.  The commission may audit the
  statement and examine records necessary to verify the annual amount
  owed.
         Sec. 398.203.  ANNUAL STIPEND TO RESIDENTS. (a) Not later
  than June 1 of each year, the administrator of the fund shall
  distribute the money received under Section 398.202, less
  reasonable audited administrative expenses not exceeding two
  percent of the annual payment, in equal per-person amounts to all
  eligible residents of the host county.
         (b)  The administrator shall provide a reasonably accessible
  method for residents to establish eligibility, including an
  alternative to electronic verification, and may rely on records
  maintained by a state agency, school district, or county subject to
  applicable confidentiality protections.
         (c)  A stipend may not depend on ownership of a homestead or
  other property, income, employment, or whether the recipient lives
  within a specified distance of the data center.  A resident is
  eligible only once for the same calendar year with respect to each
  host county.
         (d)  Unclaimed payments shall be held for three years and
  then added to the amount available for distribution in that county
  for the next payment year.  The administrator shall publish the
  annual payment obligation, total eligible residents, and amount of
  the stipend per person.
         Sec. 398.204.  PAYMENT IN ADDITION TO OTHER OBLIGATIONS. A
  payment under this subchapter is in addition to all taxes, utility
  charges, mitigation, and other obligations under law.  The payment
  may not be credited against or used to reduce an obligation
  otherwise imposed on the developer and may not be collected through
  a separately identified charge on residential electricity or water
  bills.
         Sec. 398.205.  ENFORCEMENT. (a) The attorney general, the
  commission, the county, or an eligible resident may bring an action
  for an injunction, an accounting, or the collection of amounts due
  under this subchapter.
         (b)  A court may award reasonable attorney's fees and costs
  to a prevailing claimant.  Remedies under this section are
  cumulative of other remedies provided by law.
  SUBCHAPTER F. ENFORCEMENT
         Sec. 398.251.  CIVIL PENALTY; INJUNCTION. (a) A person who
  constructs, expands, or operates a project in violation of this
  chapter is liable to the state for a civil penalty not to exceed
  $25,000 for each day of violation.  Each day constitutes a separate
  violation.
         (b)  The attorney general, on request of the commission or an
  affected political subdivision, may bring an action for an
  injunction and civil penalties.  A court may order cessation of
  unauthorized construction or operation.
         Sec. 398.252.  RULES. The commission, in consultation with
  the Public Utility Commission of Texas and the Texas Water
  Development Board, shall adopt rules to implement this chapter.
  Rules may not reduce the requirements or eligibility thresholds
  prescribed by this chapter.

         SECTION 5.  The following provisions of the Tax Code are
  repealed:
               (1)  Section 151.359;
               (2)  Section 151.3595; and
               (3)  Section 313.009.
         SECTION 6.  (a) An exemption under Section 151.359 or
  151.3595, Tax Code, to which a person is entitled on the effective
  date of this Act expires on that date.  No person is entitled to a
  refund or credit based on property purchased on or after that date
  under either repealed section.
         (b)  An application for certification as a qualifying data
  center or qualifying large data center project pending on the
  effective date of this Act is considered denied and may not be
  approved by the comptroller.
         SECTION 7.  (a) The Texas Commission on Environmental
  Quality, Public Utility Commission of Texas, and Texas Water
  Development Board shall adopt initial rules under Chapter 398,
  Health and Safety Code, as added by this Act, not later than March
  1, 2028.
         (b)  The moratorium imposed by Section 398.051, Health and
  Safety Code, as added by this Act, terminates on the second
  anniversary of the effective date of this Act, but all other
  provisions of Chapter 398 remain effective after that date.
         (c)  The requirements of Subchapter E, Chapter 398, Health
  and Safety Code, apply only to projects for which a permit is first
  issued under that chapter after the effective date of this Act.
         SECTION 8.  If any provision of this Act or its application
  to a person or circumstance is held invalid, the invalidity does not
  affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect
  without the invalid provision, and to this end the provisions of
  this Act are severable.
         SECTION 9.  This Act takes effect immediately if it receives
  a vote of two-thirds of all the members elected to each house, as
  provided by Section 39, Article III, Texas Constitution. If this
  Act does not receive the vote necessary for immediate effect, this
  Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
