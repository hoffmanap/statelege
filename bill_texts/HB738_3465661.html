By: Perez of El Paso H.B. No. 738
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to minimum standards for civil detention facilities in
  this state, inspection of those facilities on the request of a
  county or municipality, and reporting of deaths and serious
  injuries occurring at those facilities.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Subtitle B, Title 4, Health and Safety Code, is
  amended by adding Chapter 249 to read as follows:
  CHAPTER 249. CIVIL DETENTION FACILITY STANDARDS
         Sec. 249.001.  DEFINITIONS. In this chapter:
               (1)  "Child" means an individual younger than 18 years
  of age.
               (2)  "Civil detention facility" means a facility
  located in this state in which one or more individuals are held in
  custody and may not leave at will, other than custody imposed as
  punishment for or pending the disposition of a criminal charge,
  including a facility in which individuals are held:
                     (A)  under an order of civil commitment;
                     (B)  pending or following a determination of
  immigration status under federal law;
                     (C)  as a condition of receiving residential care
  or treatment; or
                     (D)  under any other civil process or authority.
               (3)  "Commission" means the Health and Human Services
  Commission.
               (4)  "Executive commissioner" means the executive
  commissioner of the commission.
               (5)  "Local health authority" has the meaning assigned
  by Section 121.021.
               (6)  "Operator" means a person that operates or manages
  a civil detention facility. The term does not include the United
  States, an agency of the United States, or an officer or employee of
  the United States acting within the scope of that person's
  employment.
         Sec. 249.002.  APPLICABILITY. (a) This chapter applies to
  each civil detention facility located in this state.
         (b)  A civil detention facility that is licensed, certified,
  or otherwise regulated under Chapter 242, 247, 248, 252, or 555 of
  this code or under Chapter 574 of this code, and that is in
  compliance with the standards applicable to the facility under that
  chapter, is considered to be in compliance with the standards
  adopted under Section 249.003.
         (c)  This chapter does not apply to the United States, an
  agency of the United States, or an officer or employee of the United
  States acting within the scope of that person's employment.
         Sec. 249.003.  MINIMUM STANDARDS. (a) The executive
  commissioner by rule shall adopt minimum standards for civil
  detention facilities relating to:
               (1)  medical care, mental health care, and the
  administration of medication;
               (2)  food, water, and nutrition;
               (3)  sanitation, hygiene, and the control of
  communicable disease;
               (4)  ventilation, heating, cooling, lighting, and the
  condition of housing and sleeping areas;
               (5)  protection of individuals in custody from
  violence, sexual abuse, and self-harm;
               (6)  communication by an individual in custody with
  family members and legal counsel;
               (7)  access to interpretation and translation
  services; and
               (8)  the care of and contact between children and the
  adults with whom they were taken into custody.
         (b)  A standard adopted under this section may not be more
  stringent than the standard applicable under this code to a
  facility described by Section 249.002(b) that provides residential
  care to individuals who may not leave the facility at will.
         (c)  This chapter does not require a license, permit, or
  certification to operate a civil detention facility, and the
  commission may not under this chapter prohibit the operation of a
  civil detention facility or determine whether or where an
  individual may be held in custody.
         Sec. 249.004.  ASSESSMENT ON REQUEST OF COUNTY OR
  MUNICIPALITY. (a) The commissioners court of a county in which a
  civil detention facility is located, or the governing body of a
  municipality in which a civil detention facility is located, may
  request that the commission conduct an assessment of:
               (1)  the facility's compliance with the standards
  adopted under Section 249.003; and
               (2)  conditions at the facility that affect the public
  health of the county or municipality.
         (b)  The commission shall conduct an assessment requested
  under Subsection (a) not later than the 30th day after the date the
  request is made.
         (c)  In conducting an assessment, the commission may enter
  the facility at reasonable times, inspect the premises, review
  records relating to the standards adopted under Section 249.003,
  and interview individuals in custody and personnel of the facility
  who consent to be interviewed.
         (d)  If the commission does not conduct an assessment within
  the period prescribed by Subsection (b), the local health authority
  for the county or municipality that made the request may conduct the
  assessment, and the commission shall reimburse the local health
  authority for the reasonable costs incurred.
         (e)  Not later than the 30th day after the date an assessment
  is completed, the commission or the local health authority, as
  applicable, shall prepare a report of the assessment, deliver the
  report to the requesting commissioners court or governing body and
  to the operator of the facility, and publish the report on the
  commission's Internet website.
         (f)  A county or municipality may not make a request under
  this section with respect to the same facility more than twice in a
  calendar year unless a death or an outbreak of communicable disease
  has been reported at the facility under Section 249.005 since the
  date of the most recent request.
         Sec. 249.005.  REPORTING. (a) Not later than 24 hours after
  the occurrence, an operator shall report to the commission and to
  the local health authority for the county in which the civil
  detention facility is located:
               (1)  the death of an individual in custody at the
  facility;
               (2)  serious bodily injury to an individual in custody
  at the facility;
               (3)  an outbreak of communicable disease at the
  facility; and
               (4)  an instance in which a child in custody at the
  facility is separated from an adult with whom the child was taken
  into custody.
         (b)  Not later than the 30th day after the last day of each
  calendar quarter, an operator shall report to the commission for
  that quarter:
               (1)  the average and maximum number of individuals held
  in custody at the facility and the facility's design capacity;
               (2)  the number of children held in custody at the
  facility and the average length of their custody; and
               (3)  the number of deaths, instances of serious bodily
  injury, and outbreaks of communicable disease at the facility.
         (c)  The commission shall publish on its Internet website the
  information reported under Subsection (b), aggregated by facility
  and by calendar quarter.
         Sec. 249.006.  CIVIL PENALTY. (a) An operator that fails to
  make a report required by Section 249.005 is liable to this state
  for a civil penalty in an amount not to exceed $1,000 for each
  report not made.
         (b)  The attorney general or the district attorney, criminal
  district attorney, or county attorney of the county in which the
  civil detention facility is located may bring an action to recover a
  civil penalty under this section.
         Sec. 249.007.  CONSTRUCTION. (a) The remedies and
  procedures provided by this chapter are in addition to any other
  remedy or procedure provided by law.
         (b)  This chapter does not create a cause of action against
  the United States and does not purport to waive the sovereign
  immunity of the United States.
         Sec. 249.008.  SEVERABILITY. If any provision of this
  chapter or its application to any person or circumstance is held
  invalid, the invalidity does not affect other provisions or
  applications of this chapter that can be given effect without the
  invalid provision or application, and to this end the provisions of
  this chapter are severable.
         SECTION 2.  Not later than September 1, 2028, the executive
  commissioner of the Health and Human Services Commission shall
  adopt the rules required by Section 249.003, Health and Safety
  Code, as added by this Act.
         SECTION 3.  Sections 249.004 and 249.005, Health and Safety
  Code, as added by this Act, apply beginning September 1, 2028.
         SECTION 4.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
