By: Bhojani H.B. No. 605
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the establishment of the Texas Department of Artificial
  Intelligence and oversight of artificial intelligence systems used
  by state agencies.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Subtitle B, Title 10, Government Code, is
  amended by adding Chapter 2064 to read as follows:
  CHAPTER 2064.  TEXAS DEPARTMENT OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
  SUBCHAPTER A.  GENERAL PROVISIONS
         Sec. 2064.001.  SHORT TITLE. This chapter may be cited as
  the Texas Department of Artificial Intelligence Act.
         Sec. 2064.002.  LEGISLATIVE FINDINGS AND PURPOSE. (a) The
  legislature finds that:
               (1)  artificial intelligence systems may improve the
  efficiency, accessibility, and quality of state services but may
  also create risks to accuracy, privacy, security, civil rights,
  fiscal stewardship, and public trust;
               (2)  the public has an interest in knowing when state
  agencies materially rely on artificial intelligence systems to
  provide services or make consequential decisions;
               (3)  independent evaluation of governmental uses of
  artificial intelligence can supplement the technical
  implementation, procurement, and cybersecurity functions assigned
  to existing agencies; and
               (4)  uniform, risk-based oversight should avoid
  unnecessary administrative burdens, duplicative reporting, and
  disclosure of confidential information.
         (b)  The purposes of this chapter are to establish an
  independent department to oversee covered artificial intelligence
  systems used by state agencies, promote responsible government
  adoption of those systems, support meaningful public transparency,
  and provide the legislature with reliable evaluations of agency
  compliance and emerging risks.
         Sec. 2064.003.  DEFINITIONS. In this chapter:
               (1)  "Artificial intelligence system" has the meaning
  assigned by Section 2054.003.
               (2)  "Board" means the governing board of the Texas
  Department of Artificial Intelligence.
               (3)  "Consequential decision" has the meaning assigned
  by Section 2054.003.
               (4)  "Covered artificial intelligence system" means an
  artificial intelligence system procured, developed, deployed, or
  used by a state agency that:
                     (A)  materially affects the delivery of a state
  service, the exercise of a governmental power, or an agency's
  operation of a critical function; or
                     (B)  is a heightened scrutiny artificial
  intelligence system.
               (5)  "Department" means the Texas Department of
  Artificial Intelligence established under this chapter.
               (6)  "Department of Information Resources" means the
  agency established under Chapter 2054.
               (7)  "Executive director" means the executive director
  of the department.
               (8)  "Heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence
  system" has the meaning assigned by Section 2054.003.
               (9)  "Material change" means a change to the intended
  use, functionality, operating environment, data inputs, or
  affected population of an artificial intelligence system that may
  substantially alter the system's risk profile or performance.
               (10)  "State agency" means a department, commission,
  board, office, council, authority, university system, institution
  of higher education as defined by Section 61.003, Education Code,
  or other agency in the executive branch of state government that is
  created by the constitution or a statute of this state.  The term
  does not include a public junior college or junior college
  district.
         Sec. 2064.004.  APPLICABILITY; CONSTRUCTION. (a) Except as
  otherwise provided by this chapter, this chapter applies to a state
  agency's procurement, development, deployment, or use of a covered
  artificial intelligence system in performing a governmental
  function.
         (b)  A university system or institution of higher education
  is subject to this chapter only with respect to its administrative
  operations and provision of governmental services.  This chapter
  does not regulate academic instruction, independently conducted
  scholarly research, or publication of research findings unless a
  resulting system is deployed to perform an administrative
  governmental function.
         (c)  This chapter does not impose a new duty on a county,
  municipality, school district, special district, or other
  political subdivision.  Duties imposed on a local government by
  Chapter 2054 or other law are not affected by this chapter.
         (d)  This chapter does not apply to the legislative or
  judicial branch of state government and may not be construed to
  impair a power expressly granted by the constitution to another
  officer or entity.
         (e)  This chapter does not authorize the department to
  regulate a private person solely because the person develops,
  markets, sells, or uses an artificial intelligence system, except
  to the extent necessary to administer a contract entered into
  directly by the department.
         (f)  This chapter may not be construed to expand the
  authority of an agency to obtain information about an individual,
  authorize surveillance, or affect a right or remedy otherwise
  provided by law.
  SUBCHAPTER B.  DEPARTMENT ESTABLISHMENT
         Sec. 2064.051.  ESTABLISHMENT; INDEPENDENCE. (a) The Texas
  Department of Artificial Intelligence is established as an
  independent state agency in the executive branch of state
  government.
         (b)  The department is separate from the Department of
  Information Resources and is governed by the board.  The department
  may not perform information technology purchasing or operate an
  artificial intelligence system on behalf of another state agency
  except as necessary to perform its own statutory functions.
         Sec. 2064.052.GOVERNING BOARD; COMPOSITION. (a) The
  department is governed by a board composed of five voting members
  appointed by the governor with the advice and consent of the senate.
         (b)  Members must be residents of this state. Appointments
  must reflect, to the extent practicable, expertise in artificial
  intelligence engineering and evaluation, privacy and civil
  liberties, state government administration, cybersecurity, public
  procurement, or public-sector risk management.
         (c)  Not more than three members may be affiliated with the
  same political party.
         (d)  Appointments must be made without regard to the race,
  color, disability, sex, religion, age, or national origin of an
  appointee.
         Sec. 2064.053.  QUALIFICATIONS; CONFLICTS OF INTEREST. (a)
  A person is eligible for appointment to the board only if the person
  possesses demonstrated professional experience or education
  relevant to a function of the department.
         (b)  A person may not serve as a board member if the person:
               (1)  is an officer, employee, or paid consultant of a
  business that, during the person's service, is party to a contract
  to furnish artificial intelligence systems directly to the
  department;
               (2)  is registered as a lobbyist under Chapter 305 and
  is compensated to advocate before the department; or
               (3)  is an officer or employee of a state agency whose
  compliance with this chapter is subject to review by the
  department.
         (c)  A member shall disclose a financial interest or other
  conflict as required by Chapter 572 and may not participate in a
  matter in which the member has a substantial personal or financial
  interest.
         (d)  The board shall adopt and enforce a written ethics and
  recusal policy that includes restrictions on the acceptance of
  gifts or employment offers from persons materially affected by a
  pending matter before the department.
         Sec. 2064.054.  TERMS; VACANCIES; PRESIDING OFFICER. (a)
  Board members serve staggered six-year terms, with the terms of one
  or two members expiring February 1 of each odd-numbered year.
         (b)  The governor shall designate a member as presiding
  officer to serve at the pleasure of the governor. The board shall
  elect a vice presiding officer.
         (c)  A vacancy is filled in the same manner as the original
  appointment for the unexpired portion of the term.
         (d)  A member may continue to serve until a successor is
  appointed and qualifies for office.
         Sec. 2064.055.  BOARD MEETINGS; PUBLIC PARTICIPATION. (a)
  The board shall meet at least once each calendar quarter and at
  other times at the call of the presiding officer or a majority of
  members.
         (b)  Board meetings are subject to Chapter 551.  The board
  shall provide a reasonable opportunity for public testimony on
  proposed statewide oversight policies, subject to lawful
  procedures for protecting confidential information.
         (c)  A majority of the members constitutes a quorum. Action
  of the board requires the affirmative vote of a majority of members
  present.
         Sec. 2064.056.  MEMBER TRAINING; EXPENSES. (a) A board
  member may not vote or deliberate on a matter before the board until
  the member completes training on this chapter, Chapter 2001, state
  ethics requirements, open meetings, public information, conflicts
  of interest, and the department's powers and limitations.
         (b)  A member is not entitled to compensation for service on
  the board but may be reimbursed for necessary and reasonable
  expenses as provided by the General Appropriations Act.
         Sec. 2064.057.  EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR. (a) The board shall
  appoint an executive director who serves at the pleasure of the
  board.
         (b)  The executive director must have at least seven years of
  professional experience in artificial intelligence governance,
  advanced technology, risk management, public administration, or a
  substantially related field, including substantial management
  experience.
         (c)  The executive director is the chief administrative
  officer of the department and is responsible for implementing board
  policies, hiring and supervising personnel, preparing the proposed
  budget, and administering department programs.
         (d)  A member of the board may not simultaneously serve as
  executive director.
         Sec. 2064.058.  BOARD AND EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR DUTIES. (a)
  The board shall establish the department's strategic direction,
  adopt rules, approve statewide oversight policies, and review
  significant findings and annual reports.
         (b)  The board may delegate an administrative or technical
  function to the executive director but may not delegate final
  rulemaking authority.
         (c)  The executive director shall establish procedures for
  receiving agency submissions, providing technical assistance,
  protecting records, and evaluating the department's performance.
  SUBCHAPTER C.  POWERS AND DUTIES
         Sec. 2064.101.  GENERAL POWERS AND DUTIES. (a) The
  department shall:
               (1)  monitor and assess state agency compliance with
  this chapter and applicable governmental artificial intelligence
  requirements;
               (2)  maintain a statewide inventory of covered
  artificial intelligence systems;
               (3)  conduct risk-based reviews and technical
  evaluations of heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence systems
  used by state agencies;
               (4)  develop standardized inventory, review, and
  public-summary formats;
               (5)  coordinate with the Department of Information
  Resources and appropriate procurement authorities on the
  governance of artificial intelligence systems acquired for state
  use;
               (6)  make recommendations to state agencies on
  mitigating identified risks and improving safeguards; and
               (7)  provide independent findings and recommendations
  to the governor and legislature.
         (b)  The department may enter into interagency agreements
  and contracts, subject to applicable purchasing and fiscal laws, to
  obtain specialized technical assistance.
         Sec. 2064.102.  STATEWIDE OVERSIGHT STANDARDS. (a) The
  board by rule shall adopt minimum procedural standards for agency
  inventories, documentation of intended uses, periodic compliance
  reviews, human oversight, public-summary preparation, and
  corrective action under this chapter.
         (b)  Standards adopted under this section must be risk-based
  and consistent with applicable federal and state law, including the
  artificial intelligence system code of ethics and minimum standards
  established by the Department of Information Resources under
  Sections 2054.702 and 2054.703, as added by S.B. 1964, Acts of the
  89th Legislature, Regular Session, 2025.
         (c)  The department may recommend changes to existing
  technical, procurement, training, or cybersecurity standards but
  may not supersede a standard assigned by law to the Department of
  Information Resources or Texas Cyber Command.
         (d)  The department shall consider the latest relevant
  guidance of the National Institute of Standards and Technology and
  other generally accepted risk management practices, without
  automatically incorporating a later revision of external guidance
  as binding law.
         Sec. 2064.103.  RESPONSIBLE AGENCY OFFICER. (a) Each state
  agency shall designate an existing officer or employee to be
  responsible for coordinating the agency's compliance with this
  chapter.  If the agency has designated an artificial intelligence
  risk officer under another law or rule, that officer may serve under
  this subsection.
         (b)  The designated officer shall coordinate the agency's
  inventory, respond to requests for compliance information, oversee
  corrective action plans, and serve as the department's primary
  contact.
         (c)  This section does not require a state agency to create a
  new full-time position.
         Sec. 2064.104.  AGENCY ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE INVENTORY.
  (a) Each state agency shall maintain an accurate internal
  inventory of its covered artificial intelligence systems in a
  standardized format prescribed by the department.
         (b)  For each covered system, the inventory must identify, to
  the extent reasonably ascertainable:
               (1)  the name or a descriptive identifier of the system
  and the agency unit responsible for its use;
               (2)  the system's governmental purpose and general
  category of data used;
               (3)  whether the system is public-facing or a
  heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence system;
               (4)  whether the system was developed internally or
  acquired from a contractor;
               (5)  the status of required assessments and safeguards,
  including meaningful human oversight; and
               (6)  the date of initial deployment and of any material
  change.
         (c)  A state agency shall submit an initial inventory and
  shall update that inventory not later than the 60th day after
  deploying a newly covered system, making a material change to a
  covered system, or retiring a covered system.
         (d)  The department by rule may establish simplified
  reporting for low-risk systems and reasonable categories of
  exclusions for incidental or nonmaterial use, but may not exclude a
  heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence system.
         (e)  The department shall use information already submitted
  under Chapter 2054 to the extent practicable and may not require
  substantially duplicative reports that provide the same
  information in a usable format.
         Sec. 2064.105.  RISK-BASED TECHNICAL EVALUATIONS. (a) The
  department shall establish a schedule of risk-based reviews that
  gives priority to heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence
  systems and systems with documented material risks to individual
  rights, essential services, security, or public funds.
         (b)  In conducting a review, the department may examine
  relevant system documentation, validation and monitoring results,
  audit trails, agency policies, procurement terms, and assessments
  already required by Section 2054.708, as added by S.B. 1964, Acts of
  the 89th Legislature, Regular Session, 2025.
         (c)  A review must consider, as applicable, system accuracy
  and limitations, human review, data governance, potential unlawful
  harm, accessibility, security controls, vendor accountability, and
  adequacy of processes for correcting erroneous outcomes.
         (d)  The department may perform testing or request an
  independent evaluation only when reasonably necessary and within
  available resources.  A review may not require access to source code
  or personally identifying information when a less intrusive means
  is adequate.
         (e)  An evaluation under this section does not excuse a state
  agency or vendor from completing an assessment required by Section
  2054.708, Government Code, and does not alter the confidentiality
  of that assessment.
         Sec. 2064.106.  PROCUREMENT COORDINATION. (a) The
  department, in consultation with the Department of Information
  Resources and the comptroller, shall publish a model checklist for
  state agency procurement and contract management involving
  heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence systems.
         (b)  The checklist must address, as appropriate, performance
  testing, data use restrictions, audit cooperation, material-change
  notice, incident reporting, subcontractor responsibilities,
  correction of inaccurate outputs, and secure disposition of agency
  information.
         (c)  A state agency considering a new procurement of a
  heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence system shall provide
  the department with a summary of the proposed governmental use
  before the agency executes the contract, in the manner prescribed
  by rule.
         (d)  Not later than the 30th day after receiving a
  substantially complete summary under Subsection (c), the
  department may provide written risk recommendations.  A failure to
  provide recommendations within that period does not prohibit an
  otherwise lawful procurement.
         (e)  This section does not transfer purchasing authority
  from the comptroller, Department of Information Resources, or a
  procuring state agency and may not be construed to create an
  exemption from competitive procurement requirements.
         Sec. 2064.107.  TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE; BEST PRACTICES.  The
  department may provide state agencies with nonbinding model
  policies, evaluation methods, sample reporting forms, and risk
  mitigation assistance.  The department shall coordinate these
  resources with existing services provided by the Department of
  Information Resources and the Texas Artificial Intelligence
  Council to avoid duplication.
         Sec. 2064.108.  LIMITATION ON POWERS.  (a) The department
  may not independently acquire or deploy an artificial intelligence
  system on behalf of another state agency, grant a license to a
  private business to develop or use artificial intelligence, or
  assess a civil penalty except as expressly authorized by another
  law.
         (b)  The department may not require disclosure of privileged
  information to the public or interfere with an ongoing criminal
  investigation, judicial proceeding, or legally protected
  public-safety function.
  SUBCHAPTER D.  RULEMAKING AND COMPLIANCE
         Sec. 2064.151.  RULEMAKING AUTHORITY. (a) The board may
  adopt rules necessary to implement and administer this chapter,
  including rules governing inventories, compliance reviews, public
  reporting, and corrective action procedures.
         (b)  A rule adopted under this chapter is subject to Chapter
  2001.  The department shall publish proposed rules, consider public
  comment and fiscal effects, and provide opportunities for public
  participation as required by law.
         (c)  The department may not adopt a rule that expands the
  application of this chapter to a private person or political
  subdivision or conflicts with express authority granted to another
  state agency by law.
         Sec. 2064.152.  LIMITATIONS ON RULEMAKING; CONSULTATION.
  (a) Before proposing a rule that materially affects procurement,
  information technology operations, or cybersecurity, the
  department shall consult with the Department of Information
  Resources, comptroller, or Texas Cyber Command, as applicable.
         (b)  In developing a proposed rule, the department shall
  assess the administrative and fiscal burdens on state agencies of
  different sizes and shall favor practicable reporting standards,
  risk-based review schedules, and reuse of existing submissions.
         (c)  The department shall provide in the rulemaking record a
  written explanation of how the rule avoids unnecessary duplication
  of a requirement imposed under Chapter 2054 or 2063.
         Sec. 2064.153.  COMPLIANCE REVIEWS. (a) The department may
  conduct a periodic or risk-based review of a state agency's
  compliance with this chapter and rules adopted under this chapter.
         (b)  The department shall provide the agency written notice
  of the review's scope and the categories of records reasonably
  necessary for the review, except when advance notice would
  materially impair an authorized audit of an imminent risk.
         (c)  A state agency shall cooperate with a review and may
  provide an existing report or assessment in lieu of a new submission
  when the existing document contains the required information.
         (d)  The department shall use secure procedures for
  receiving and examining confidential information and shall limit
  access to personnel with a documented need to know.
         Sec. 2064.154.  FINDINGS; CORRECTIVE ACTION PLAN. (a) If a
  compliance review identifies a material failure to comply with this
  chapter or a rule adopted under this chapter, the executive
  director shall provide the agency written findings stating the
  legal requirement, the factual basis of the findings, and
  recommended corrective action.
         (b)  Not later than the 30th day after receiving the
  findings, the agency may submit a response and supporting
  information.
         (c)  If the executive director determines that a material
  deficiency remains, the agency shall submit a proposed corrective
  action plan not later than the 45th day after receipt of that
  determination.  The plan must identify responsible officials,
  proposed steps, and reasonable completion dates.
         (d)  The department and agency shall attempt to resolve
  disputes concerning feasibility or time for compliance.  The
  executive director shall approve a plan that reasonably addresses
  the deficiency and may require periodic status updates.
         (e)  A corrective action plan may not require an agency to
  take action prohibited by another law or to spend money not lawfully
  available for that purpose.  The agency shall identify any
  statutory or appropriation constraint in its plan.
         Sec. 2064.155.  REVIEW OF COMPLIANCE DETERMINATION. (a) A
  state agency may request board review of a final material
  deficiency determination or a required corrective action plan not
  later than the 30th day after the determination or requirement is
  issued.
         (b)  The board shall consider a written statement from the
  affected agency and issue a reasoned written decision not later
  than the 60th day after receiving a request for review, unless the
  agency agrees to an extension.
         (c)  The department shall provide any hearing or other
  procedure required by Chapter 2001 when the matter is a contested
  case under law.  This section does not independently create a right
  to judicial review beyond that otherwise provided by law.
         (d)  The board shall maintain a confidential version of its
  findings when publication would reveal information protected by law
  and shall prepare a nonconfidential summary when practicable.
         Sec. 2064.156.  UNRESOLVED DEFICIENCIES; URGENT RISKS. (a)
  If a state agency does not submit or materially fails to implement
  an approved corrective action plan, the department shall notify the
  agency's governing authority, the governor, and the Legislative
  Budget Board and may include a nonconfidential description in its
  annual report.
         (b)  On identifying an imminent and substantial risk of
  serious unlawful harm, material exposure of confidential
  information, or material disruption of an essential governmental
  service, the executive director may issue a written urgent-risk
  notice to the agency and appropriate oversight entities.  The
  notice may recommend suspension or limitation of the affected
  system but does not itself authorize the department to suspend
  another agency's statutory operations.
         (c)  The department shall coordinate any cybersecurity
  incident referral with Texas Cyber Command and any alleged
  violation of law within the attorney general's jurisdiction with
  the attorney general.
         Sec. 2064.157.  NO PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION OR INDEPENDENT
  PENALTY. This chapter does not create a private cause of action, a
  new basis for civil or criminal liability, or independent authority
  for the department to assess an administrative or civil penalty.
  SUBCHAPTER E.  TRANSPARENCY AND REPORTING
         Sec. 2064.201.  PUBLIC ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE REGISTRY.
  (a) The department shall establish and maintain on its publicly
  accessible Internet website a searchable registry of covered
  artificial intelligence systems reported by state agencies.
         (b)  Subject to Section 2064.205, for each system the public
  registry must include, to the extent practicable:
               (1)  the responsible state agency and the system's name
  or general descriptive identifier;
               (2)  the system's governmental purpose and whether it
  is public-facing or a heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence
  system;
               (3)  a general description of the system's role in any
  consequential decision and whether human review is available;
               (4)  the status of required public impact summaries;
  and
               (5)  a contact method for public inquiries about the
  agency's use of the system.
         (c)  The department shall provide an accessible method to
  search the registry by agency, purpose, and system category and
  shall update public entries at least quarterly.
         (d)  The registry is intended to supplement, and does not
  replace, disclosure required under Sections 2054.707 and 2054.711
  or any other law.
         Sec. 2064.202.  NONCONFIDENTIAL IMPACT SUMMARIES. (a) For
  each heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence system that a
  state agency deploys or uses, the agency shall prepare a
  plain-language public impact summary independent of any
  confidential assessment required by Section 2054.708.
         (b)  The public impact summary must describe, in general
  terms:
               (1)  the governmental purpose of the system;
               (2)  the categories of individuals or services
  materially affected;
               (3)  the principal categories of foreseeable risk and
  general measures used to mitigate those risks;
               (4)  the existence of meaningful human oversight and a
  method for requesting review or correction of an alleged error, if
  review or correction is available by law; and
               (5)  the date of the most recent material update.
         (c)  A state agency shall furnish the summary to the
  department for publication in the registry before initial
  deployment or, for a system already in use, within the period
  established by transition rules.
         (d)  A public impact summary may not include any part of an
  assessment made confidential by Section 2054.708, source code,
  protected personal information, trade secrets, or information
  exempt from public disclosure under other law.  A summary must
  instead use general descriptions sufficient for meaningful public
  understanding without exposing protected information.
         (e)  The agency shall update a public impact summary after a
  material change affecting its accuracy and at least once every two
  years while the system remains in operation.
         Sec. 2064.203.  PUBLIC INQUIRIES; CORRECTIONS. (a) The
  department shall provide an accessible process for the public to
  notify the department of a material error in the registry or report
  concerns about an agency's use of a covered artificial intelligence
  system.
         (b)  The department shall refer an inquiry about a particular
  agency's service, decision, or alleged error to the responsible
  agency or appropriate oversight office and may use aggregated
  inquiry information to prioritize reviews.
         (c)  This section does not create an administrative appeal
  from a governmental decision or extend a deadline for judicial or
  administrative review under other law.
         Sec. 2064.204.  ANNUAL REPORT. (a)  Not later than November
  1 of each year, the department shall submit to the governor,
  lieutenant governor, speaker of the house of representatives,
  Legislative Budget Board, and appropriate standing committees of
  the legislature a report concerning the preceding state fiscal
  year.
         (b)  The report must include:
               (1)  an aggregate inventory of covered artificial
  intelligence systems and systems classified as heightened
  scrutiny;
               (2)  the number and categories of reviews conducted and
  the principal compliance findings;
               (3)  the number of corrective action plans requested
  and the status of corrective measures;
               (4)  material trends in public concerns, risk
  mitigation, agency practices, and use of public funds;
               (5)  an accounting of department expenditures and
  performance against measures adopted under Section 2064.304;
               (6)  identified duplication or gaps in existing AI
  oversight authority and recommendations for statutory changes; and
               (7)  a summary of cooperative activities with the
  Department of Information Resources, Texas Artificial Intelligence
  Council, attorney general, and Texas Cyber Command.
         (c)  The department shall publish a version of the report on
  its website that omits information confidential or excepted from
  disclosure under law.
         Sec. 2064.205.  SECURITY; CONFIDENTIALITY; INFORMATION
  SHARING. (a) The department shall protect confidential
  information obtained under this chapter using administrative,
  physical, and technical safeguards consistent with applicable law
  and statewide cybersecurity requirements.
         (b)  Information that is confidential under Section 2054.708
  or other law remains confidential when provided to the department.  
  This chapter does not require publication of that information or
  create an exception to disclosure of information otherwise public
  under Chapter 552.
         (c)  Before publishing a registry entry, impact summary, or
  report, the department shall consult with the reporting agency as
  necessary to remove details that would disclose personal
  identifying information, security vulnerabilities, protected
  investigations, trade secrets, or other information confidential
  by law.
         (d)  The department may share confidential information with
  another state agency only when authorized by law, only for a
  governmental purpose within that agency's authority, and under
  controls that preserve applicable confidentiality.
         (e)  The department shall adopt a written retention schedule
  in accordance with Chapter 441 and shall not retain copies of
  underlying sensitive technical data longer than reasonably
  necessary to perform an authorized review, subject to applicable
  records laws.
  SUBCHAPTER F.  INTERAGENCY COORDINATION
         Sec. 2064.251.  DEPARTMENT OF INFORMATION RESOURCES. (a)
  The Department of Information Resources retains its duties under
  Chapter 2054, including the governmental artificial intelligence
  code of ethics, minimum standards for heightened scrutiny
  artificial intelligence systems, education and training,
  generative artificial intelligence implementation assistance, and
  information technology purchasing and infrastructure functions
  assigned by law.
         (b)  The department shall lead the independent inventory,
  compliance review, public-registry, and public-impact-summary
  functions established by this chapter.
         (c)  The departments shall enter into a memorandum of
  understanding that establishes complementary responsibilities, a
  secure process for exchanging information, coordinated reporting
  formats, and procedures for resolving inconsistent administrative
  guidance without changing either agency's statutory powers.
         (d)  The department may recommend changes to the Department
  of Information Resources' AI code of ethics or minimum standards.
  The Department of Information Resources retains rulemaking
  authority assigned to it by Chapter 2054.
         Sec. 2064.252.  TEXAS ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE COUNCIL. (a)
  The department shall consult the Texas Artificial Intelligence
  Council established under Chapter 554, Business & Commerce Code, on
  statewide policy recommendations and emerging risks within the
  council's statutory purposes.
         (b)  The department may furnish the council a
  nonconfidential summary of trends identified in state agency
  reviews and shall consider council recommendations when preparing
  its annual report.
         (c)  This chapter does not alter the council's advisory
  status or authorize the council to issue binding standards or
  override the operation of a state agency.
         Sec. 2064.253.  ATTORNEY GENERAL. (a) The department may
  refer evidence of a potential violation of law to the attorney
  general and may provide technical assistance upon lawful request.
         (b)  This chapter does not alter the attorney general's
  exclusive enforcement authority under Chapter 552, Business &
  Commerce Code, or confer on the department the attorney general's
  investigative or litigation powers.
         (c)  The department shall protect confidential and
  privileged materials furnished to or received from the attorney
  general in accordance with law.
         Sec. 2064.254.  TEXAS CYBER COMMAND. (a) The department
  shall coordinate with Texas Cyber Command established under Chapter
  2063 on cybersecurity risks involving covered artificial
  intelligence systems.
         (b)  Texas Cyber Command retains authority over statewide
  cybersecurity programs, security incident response, and
  cybersecurity standards assigned to the command by law.
         (c)  The department shall promptly refer a report of a
  material cybersecurity incident within the command's jurisdiction
  to the command and shall avoid requiring conflicting cybersecurity
  incident reports.
         Sec. 2064.255.  COORDINATION WITH OTHER AGENCIES. (a) The
  department shall coordinate its activities with the comptroller,
  state auditor, Legislative Budget Board, and other state agencies
  having related oversight or procurement duties when appropriate.
         (b)  Nothing in this chapter makes the department a
  substitute for an agency's legally assigned decision-making
  authority, responsibility to protect records, or duty to administer
  its own contracts.
         (c)  The department may offer assistance to a local
  government on request but may not impose a requirement on a local
  government under this chapter.
  SUBCHAPTER G.  ADMINISTRATION AND SUNSET
         Sec. 2064.301.  FUNDING. (a) The department shall operate
  using money appropriated to it by the legislature and other money
  lawfully available for its purposes.
         (b)  Subject to other law, the department may accept gifts,
  grants, and donations for a public purpose within its statutory
  authority.  The department may not accept a gift or grant that would
  compromise its independence or permit a donor to influence a review
  or regulatory determination concerning the donor.
         (c)  The department may not impose a regulatory fee or
  assessment on a private person or state agency unless expressly
  authorized by law.
         Sec. 2064.302.  PERSONNEL; ORGANIZATION. (a) The executive
  director may employ and discharge personnel necessary to carry out
  this chapter, subject to applicable appropriations and state
  employment law.
         (b)  The department shall maintain professional capacity in
  AI evaluation, policy and law, privacy, security, audit and risk
  management, and public-sector administration, either through
  personnel or lawful interagency agreements.
         (c)  The executive director shall adopt a personnel policy
  addressing conflicts of interest, protection of confidential
  information, and separation between evaluators and vendors whose
  systems are being reviewed.
         Sec. 2064.303.  ADMINISTRATIVE SERVICES. (a) The
  department may enter into interagency agreements for shared
  administrative, information technology, human resources,
  accounting, procurement, legal support, and records management
  services to avoid unnecessary duplication of infrastructure.
         (b)  An agreement under this section may provide for
  reimbursement of actual costs and must preserve the department's
  independent responsibility for policy and compliance
  determinations.
         Sec. 2064.304.  PERFORMANCE MEASURES. (a) The board shall
  adopt measurable annual performance indicators, including timely
  inventory reporting, completed reviews, implementation of
  corrective action plans, accuracy and accessibility of public
  registry entries, time required for agency assistance, and costs
  per review.
         (b)  The department shall provide performance information to
  the Legislative Budget Board and the state auditor in the form
  required by law and include a summary in the annual report required
  by Section 2064.204.
         (c)  The board shall periodically evaluate whether reporting
  and oversight costs are proportionate to the risks addressed by the
  department's activities.
         Sec. 2064.305.  SUNSET PROVISION. The Texas Department of
  Artificial Intelligence is subject to Chapter 325 (Texas Sunset
  Act).  Unless continued in existence as provided by that chapter,
  the department is abolished September 1, 2039.
         Sec. 2064.306.  PERIODIC REVIEW OF DUPLICATION. Not later
  than December 1, 2030, and every four years thereafter, the board
  shall submit to the governor, the legislature, and the Sunset
  Advisory Commission an evaluation of the extent to which the
  department's functions overlap with functions of the Department of
  Information Resources, Texas Artificial Intelligence Council, or
  Texas Cyber Command and recommendations for eliminating any
  unnecessary duplication.
