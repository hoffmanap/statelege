By: Bhojani H.B. No. 585
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the requirements for artificial intelligence
  operations of state agencies, establishing artificial intelligence
  divisions.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Chapter 2054, Government Code, is amended by
  adding Subchapter T to read as follows:
  SUBCHAPTER T.  STATE AGENCY ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE GOVERNANCE AND
  PUBLIC DISCLOSURE
         Sec. 2054.751.  DEFINITIONS. In this subchapter:
               (1)  "Artificial intelligence division" means the
  organizational unit established by a state agency under Section
  2054.752.
               (2)  "Artificial intelligence risk officer" means the
  employee designated under Section 2054.753.
               (3)  "Material change" means a change to an artificial
  intelligence system, the state data used by the system, or the
  intended use of the system that may materially affect the system's
  accuracy, security, privacy risks, legal compliance, or effects on
  individuals.
               (4)  "Public artificial intelligence impact statement"
  means the public-facing statement required by Section 2054.758 and
  does not include an impact assessment prepared under Section
  2054.708.
         Sec. 2054.752.  ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE DIVISION REQUIRED.
  (a)  Each state agency shall establish and maintain an artificial
  intelligence division within the agency to oversee the procurement,
  development, deployment, and use of artificial intelligence
  systems by the agency or on behalf of the agency.
         (b)  The division shall coordinate compliance with this
  subchapter and other applicable law concerning artificial
  intelligence systems, including applicable rules adopted by the
  department.
         (c)  The executive head of the agency shall designate the
  personnel and resources necessary to carry out the division's
  duties.  The agency may assign existing employees to the division,
  and the division may consist of a single employee.  This section
  does not require the creation of a new full-time equivalent
  position.
         (d)  A state agency that maintains an existing
  organizational unit performing the duties required by this
  subchapter may designate that unit as the agency's artificial
  intelligence division.
         (e)  The agency remains responsible for compliance with this
  subchapter regardless of whether a system is furnished or operated
  by a contractor.
         Sec. 2054.753.  ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE RISK OFFICER.  (a)  
  Each state agency shall designate an employee of the artificial
  intelligence division to serve as the agency's artificial
  intelligence risk officer.  The agency may assign this duty to an
  employee who performs other duties.
         (b)  The artificial intelligence risk officer shall:
               (1)  promote the ethical procurement, development,
  deployment, and use of artificial intelligence systems, consistent
  with the agency's code of ethics and the Artificial Intelligence
  Risk Management Framework published by the National Institute of
  Standards and Technology;
               (2)  oversee the inventory and ongoing evaluation of
  agency artificial intelligence systems;
               (3)  ensure the completion of risk assessments and
  impact assessments required for heightened scrutiny artificial
  intelligence systems;
               (4)  review risk assessments and approve or deny
  deployment of heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence systems
  as provided by Section 2054.756; and
               (5)  coordinate employee training, incident response,
  vendor compliance, record retention, and preparation of public
  artificial intelligence impact statements.
         (c)  The artificial intelligence risk officer shall have
  direct access to the agency's executive head or the executive head's
  designee regarding material artificial intelligence risks and
  compliance deficiencies.
         Sec. 2054.754.  AGENCY ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE CODE OF
  ETHICS. (a)  Each state agency shall adopt and maintain a written
  artificial intelligence code of ethics that incorporates and is no
  less protective than the code of ethics established by the
  department under this chapter.
         (b)  The agency's code of ethics must establish policies and
  procedures addressing:
               (1)  human oversight and control, including
  appropriate review of system inputs and outputs and the ability to
  pause, restrict, or disable a system that produces harmful or
  inaccurate results;
               (2)  fairness, accuracy, and protection of legally
  protected rights, including data governance practices and
  monitoring throughout the system's life cycle;
               (3)  transparency, including disclosure of
  public-facing artificial intelligence interactions and
  consequential uses and a prohibition on representing an artificial
  intelligence system as a human being;
               (4)  data privacy and security, including data
  minimization, authorized uses of personally identifying
  information, access controls, security monitoring, and records
  retention;
               (5)  public and internal redress, including a point of
  contact for complaints and procedures to identify and remedy
  adverse effects of artificial intelligence systems;
               (6)  accountability for agency and contractor conduct,
  including appropriate contract terms and timely disclosure by
  vendors of known vulnerabilities and available remedies;
               (7)  employee training on lawful, accurate, safe, and
  ethical use of artificial intelligence systems; and
               (8)  regular evaluations of artificial intelligence
  systems and documentation of identified deficiencies and
  improvements.
         (c)  Each state agency shall make its code of ethics
  available to agency employees and publish the code on its public
  Internet website.  The code must be reviewed at least annually and
  revised as necessary to remain consistent with department rules and
  applicable law.
         Sec. 2054.755.  INVENTORY, MONITORING, AND RECORDS.  (a)  
  Each state agency shall maintain an internal inventory of
  artificial intelligence systems the agency procures, develops,
  deploys, or uses, including systems used by a contractor on the
  agency's behalf. The inventory must identify each system's intended
  purpose, responsible agency unit, and whether the system is
  public-facing or subject to heightened scrutiny.
         (b)  The agency shall establish methods for regularly
  evaluating each deployed artificial intelligence system for
  accuracy, performance, safety, privacy, and continued compliance
  with the agency's code of ethics.  An evaluation must occur at least
  annually and after a material change affecting a heightened
  scrutiny artificial intelligence system.
         (c)  The agency shall document evaluations, corrective
  measures, system modifications, and relevant complaints and shall
  retain records, including system inputs and outputs when
  applicable, in accordance with state records retention
  requirements and other applicable law.
         Sec. 2054.756.  RISK ASSESSMENTS FOR HEIGHTENED SCRUTINY
  ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEMS. (a)  Before a state agency
  develops, procures, deploys, or uses a heightened scrutiny
  artificial intelligence system, and when a material change is made
  to the system, the agency shall complete a written risk assessment
  evaluating the probability and severity of potential harm.
         (b)  The risk assessment must consider and document:
               (1)  known security risks and available measures to
  mitigate those risks;
               (2)  performance measures, including accuracy,
  relevance, and operational efficiency;
               (3)  transparency measures, including available
  information about the system's decision-making methods, training
  data, and inputs and outputs used to monitor its decisions;
               (4)  the adequacy of human oversight, privacy
  protections, and data governance; and
               (5)  any additional safeguards necessary to address
  reasonably foreseeable risks.
         (c)  Before deployment, the artificial intelligence risk
  officer shall review the assessment and approve or deny deployment
  based on the documented risks and mitigation measures.  The officer
  shall notify the executive head of the agency or the executive
  head's designee of a decision to approve deployment.  An agency may
  require additional executive approval but may not dispense with the
  officer's review.
         (d)  A state agency shall retain each risk assessment and
  related records for the period required by the applicable records
  retention schedule.
         Sec. 2054.757.  IMPACT ASSESSMENTS FOR HEIGHTENED SCRUTINY
  ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEMS. (a)  Before deploying or using a
  heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence system, and when a
  material change is made to the system, the state data used by the
  system, or the intended use of the system, a state agency shall
  conduct or obtain an impact assessment consistent with Section
  2054.708.
         (b)  The impact assessment must include:
               (1)  a description of the system, including its model,
  available information regarding training data, and intended use;
               (2)  a description of the agency's planned use and
  identification of the employees responsible for deployment,
  monitoring, and evaluation;
               (3)  whether the system will process or store
  personally identifying information and, if so, whether that
  information may be used to train the system;
               (4)  reasonably foreseeable risks of harm in violation
  of state or federal law, including unlawful discrimination, and
  measures to mitigate those risks;
               (5)  known system limitations;
               (6)  procedures and intervals for monitoring outputs
  for accuracy and harmful effects; and
               (7)  the retention period for inputs and outputs and
  the method for deleting records when authorized by law.
         (c)  At the request of an agency, a vendor may prepare an
  impact assessment on behalf of the agency.  The agency shall obtain
  and review the completed assessment and retain it as an agency
  record.  The agency shall provide the assessment to the department
  on request, subject to Section 2054.708.
         (d)  An impact assessment under this section is subject to
  the confidentiality protections of Section 2054.708.  An agency may
  not publish the confidential assessment as a substitute for the
  public statement required by Section 2054.758.
         Sec. 2054.758.  PUBLIC ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IMPACT
  STATEMENT.  (a)  Each state agency shall prepare, maintain, and
  publish a public artificial intelligence impact statement
  describing the agency's procurement, development, deployment, and
  use of artificial intelligence systems.
         (b)  The public artificial intelligence impact statement
  must contain, in plain language:
               (1)  a description of the agency's artificial
  intelligence systems, or groups of substantially similar systems
  used solely for internal administrative functions, and their
  general purposes;
               (2)  identification of each system that is
  public-facing or is a heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence
  system, including the public services or consequential decisions
  the system supports;
               (3)  a summary, for each system described by
  Subdivision (2), of material known limitations and reasonably
  foreseeable risks involving accuracy, fairness, privacy, security,
  and legally protected rights;
               (4)  a summary of safeguards, including human
  oversight, accuracy monitoring, data protection measures, and
  mitigation of identified risks;
               (5)  the identity of the agency unit responsible for
  the system, the date the statement was last updated, and the general
  schedule for future review; and
               (6)  instructions for obtaining information or
  submitting a complaint, including a publicly accessible method to
  contact the agency's artificial intelligence risk officer or a
  designated representative.
         (c)  A state agency shall publish the statement in a clear
  and conspicuous manner on the agency's primary public Internet
  website.  The agency shall:
               (1)  provide a readily identifiable link labeled
  "Artificial Intelligence Impact Statement" on the agency's
  homepage in the primary navigation or another prominently displayed
  location;
               (2)  ensure that the statement is available without a
  fee, account registration, or password and is readily accessible to
  individuals with disabilities; and
               (3)  present the statement in clear, understandable
  language and not solely within a privacy policy, terms of use, or
  other unrelated document.
         (d)  The statement must be published before the agency
  initially deploys a public-facing or heightened scrutiny
  artificial intelligence system.  The agency shall update the
  statement not later than the 30th day after a material change to a
  disclosed system or the deployment of another artificial
  intelligence system and shall review the statement at least
  annually.
         (e)  A state agency that does not deploy or use an artificial
  intelligence system shall publish a statement to that effect and
  shall review the statement at least annually.
         (f)  A public artificial intelligence impact statement under
  this section is a separate public disclosure and is not an impact
  assessment under Section 2054.708.  An agency shall not include in
  the public statement an impact assessment made confidential by that
  section, personally identifying information, information made
  confidential by other law, information that would materially
  compromise cybersecurity, or information protected as a trade
  secret under applicable law.  The agency shall provide a
  nonconfidential, general description of a withheld risk or
  safeguard when the description can be made without revealing
  protected information.
         Sec. 2054.759.  ACCEPTABLE USE POLICIES; TRAINING; VENDORS.  
  (a)  Before deploying or using a heightened scrutiny artificial
  intelligence system, each state agency shall identify approved uses
  and system limitations and adopt a written acceptable use policy
  that prohibits unapproved uses.
         (b)  Each agency shall provide employees and contractors who
  access, use, or manage a heightened scrutiny artificial
  intelligence system with training on the policy, material known
  risks, and appropriate mitigation practices before authorizing
  such access or use.
         (c)  A contract under which a vendor deploys or operates a
  heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence system for a state
  agency must require the vendor to implement the Artificial
  Intelligence Risk Management Framework published by the National
  Institute of Standards and Technology or a comparable risk
  management framework, disclose known material vulnerabilities and
  available remedies in a timely manner, and cooperate with required
  risk and impact assessments.
         Sec. 2054.760.  DEPARTMENT GUIDANCE; COMPLIANCE.  (a)  The
  department shall develop and make available a model public
  artificial intelligence impact statement and guidance for
  implementing this subchapter.  The department may adopt rules
  necessary to implement this subchapter, including rules concerning
  accessible Internet publication and standardized disclosures.
         (b)  Not later than September 30 of each year, each state
  agency shall certify to the department that the agency maintains
  the division, risk officer, code of ethics, assessments, and
  published statement required by this subchapter.  The certification
  must identify the Internet address at which the agency's statement
  is published.
         (c)  If the department determines that a state agency has not
  complied with this subchapter, the department shall notify the
  agency's executive head and provide a reasonable opportunity to
  correct the deficiency. The department may publish a list of
  agencies that have not corrected identified deficiencies after
  notice.
         Sec. 2054.761.  CONSTRUCTION. (a)  This subchapter is
  cumulative of, and does not limit, duties imposed by another
  provision of this chapter or by rules adopted under this chapter.
         (b)  This subchapter does not require disclosure of
  information that is confidential or excepted from required
  disclosure under Chapter 552 or other law and does not modify the
  confidentiality of an impact assessment under Section 2054.708.
         (c)  This subchapter does not modify the obligations of local
  governments under other law or create a private cause of action.
         SECTION 2.  (a)  Not later than March 1, 2028, each state
  agency shall establish the artificial intelligence division,
  designate the artificial intelligence risk officer, and adopt the
  code of ethics required by Subchapter T, Chapter 2054, Government
  Code, as added by this Act.
         (b)  Not later than September 1, 2028, each state agency
  shall publish its initial public artificial intelligence impact
  statement under Section 2054.758, Government Code, as added by this
  Act, covering systems in operation on that date.  For a
  public-facing or heightened scrutiny artificial intelligence
  system first deployed on or after September 1, 2028, the agency
  shall publish the required information before deployment.
         (c)  Not later than March 1, 2028, the Department of
  Information Resources shall make available the model statement and
  guidance required by Section 2054.760, Government Code, as added by
  this Act.
         (d)  This section does not delay or suspend any requirement
  concerning an artificial intelligence system that applied to a
  state agency before the effective date of this Act.
         SECTION 3.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
