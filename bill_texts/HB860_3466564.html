By: Landgraf H.B. No. 860
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to the regulation of mobile food vendors and temporary
  food event operations; requiring an occupational license;
  authorizing fees; authorizing an administrative penalty; repealing
  Chapter 437A, Health and Safety Code.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  This Act may be cited as the Lone Star License to
  Grill Act.
         SECTION 2.  Chapter 437B, Health and Safety Code, is amended
  to read as follows:
  CHAPTER 437B. MOBILE FOOD VENDORS
  SUBCHAPTER A. GENERAL PROVISIONS
         Sec. 437B.001.  DEFINITIONS.  In this chapter:
               (1)  "Department" means the Department of State Health
  Services.
               (2)  "Executive commissioner" means the executive
  commissioner of the Health and Human Services Commission.
               (3)  "Food vending vehicle" means a vehicle, trailer,
  cart, or other unit that is readily movable and in or from which
  food or beverages are prepared, stored, sold, or served for
  immediate consumption. The term does not include a unit operated
  solely under a valid temporary food event permit unless Section
  437B.251 requires a reciprocal mobile food vendor license for the
  operation. A unit does not cease to be readily movable solely
  because it operates for an extended period at one location or uses a
  utility connection if it can be disconnected and moved without
  permanent structural alteration or material delay.
               (4)  "Home licensing authority" means the department or
  local health department through which a mobile food vendor applies
  for and maintains a reciprocal mobile food vendor license.
               (5)  "License holder" means a person who holds a
  reciprocal mobile food vendor license or provisional reciprocal
  mobile food vendor license.
               (6)  "Local authority" means a municipality, county,
  public health district, special district or authority, or other
  political subdivision of this state.
               (7)  "Local health department" means a local
  governmental entity authorized by law to enforce state food-safety
  laws and rules.
               (8)  "Mobile food health requirement" means a permit,
  license, inspection, fee, standard, documentation requirement, or
  other requirement concerning the food safety or sanitary operation
  of a mobile food vendor or food vending vehicle.  The term does not
  include a non-health requirement unless imposed or enforced to
  regulate food safety or avoid a limitation imposed by this chapter.
               (9)  "Mobile food vendor" means a person who sells or
  serves food or beverages from a food vending vehicle for immediate
  consumption. The term does not include:
                     (A)  a person operating solely under a valid
  temporary food event permit unless Section 437B.251 requires a
  reciprocal mobile food vendor license for the operation;
                     (B)  a cottage food production operation as
  defined by Section 437.001; or
                     (C)  another operation exempted by law or a rule
  adopted under this chapter.
               (10)  "Prepackaged food" means any commercially
  labeled and processed food that is prepackaged to prevent direct
  human contact with the food product on distribution from the
  manufacturer, food facility, or other approved source.
               (11)  "Reciprocal mobile food vendor license" means a
  license issued under this chapter for a specific food vending
  vehicle that is valid statewide.
               (12)  "Temporary food event" means an event of limited
  duration, including a fair, festival, farmers market, charitable or
  civic event, seasonal event, or similar event prescribed by a rule
  adopted under this chapter.
         Sec. 437B.002.  STATEWIDE RECIPROCITY; PREEMPTION.  (a)  
  Except as provided by this chapter, a person may not operate a food
  vending vehicle as a mobile food vendor unless the person holds a
  valid reciprocal mobile food vendor license for the vehicle or
  another operating authorization issued under this chapter for the
  operation.
         (b)  A reciprocal mobile food vendor license is valid
  statewide. A local authority may not require a license holder to
  obtain or pay for a separate permit or license that constitutes a
  mobile food health requirement, undergo a duplicative routine
  health inspection, or comply with a mobile food health requirement
  that differs from the statewide health standards adopted under this
  chapter.
         (c)  A local authority may enforce a non-health requirement
  otherwise authorized by law but may not administer or enforce the
  requirement for the purpose or with the effect of imposing a mobile
  food health requirement prohibited by this chapter.
         (d)  A fee for a non-health requirement must be reasonably
  related to the cost of administering the requirement and may not be
  based on license classification, menu, food handling processes, or
  health inspection status. If the requirement applies to similarly
  situated non-food vendors, the fee charged to a mobile food vendor
  may not exceed the fee charged to those vendors.
         (e)  Notwithstanding Chapter 437 or Section 121.0035, this
  chapter exclusively governs health-related licensing, permitting,
  inspections, fees, and administrative enforcement for a mobile food
  vendor or food vending vehicle required to hold a license or other
  operating authorization under this chapter.
         Sec. 437B.003.  RULES. (a)  The executive commissioner
  shall adopt rules necessary to implement this chapter, including
  rules establishing uniform statewide health standards, risk-based
  classification of each food vending vehicle as Type I, Type II, or
  Type III in ascending order of food-safety risk, and uniform
  application, renewal, inspection, and reporting forms and
  procedures.  A substantive health or safety requirement adopted
  under this chapter must be reasonably necessary to address a
  material health or safety risk.
         (b)  Rules adopted under this chapter may not:
               (1)  regulate a mobile food vendor's hours or location
  of operation except as reasonably necessary to address a health
  risk governed by this chapter;
               (2)  require an operational handwashing sink in a food
  vending vehicle that dispenses only prepackaged food;
               (3)  prohibit extended operation at one location or the
  use of a utility connection solely because of the duration or
  connection if the vehicle can be disconnected and moved without
  permanent structural alteration or material delay and the operation
  otherwise complies with applicable law;
               (4)  require a mobile food vendor to contract with or
  use a particular commissary, food establishment, or service
  provider if the vendor demonstrates compliance through another
  lawful method;
               (5)  restrict a mobile food vendor's propane capacity
  below the capacity state law allows for commercial vehicles; or
               (6)  require a mobile food vendor to provide
  fingerprints or submit to continuous or real-time location
  tracking.
         Sec. 437B.004.  DEPARTMENT REVIEW OF LOCAL REQUIREMENTS.
  (a)  A person or local authority directly affected by a local
  requirement may request that the department determine whether the
  requirement conflicts with or is preempted by this chapter.
         (b)  The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  procedures for making a determination under this section. The
  procedures must address when a request is complete, provide the
  requester and each affected local authority with notice and a
  reasonable opportunity to respond, specify reasonable deadlines
  for written determinations, and provide for expedited interim
  relief when a local requirement prevents or materially delays
  lawful operation.
         (c)  An affected local authority shall comply with a final or
  interim department determination to the extent stated in the
  determination unless the determination is stayed, modified, or
  reversed by a court.
         (d)  A person or local authority aggrieved by a final
  determination may, not later than the 30th day after receiving
  notice of the determination, seek declaratory or injunctive relief
  in a district court in Travis County or a county in which the
  requirement applies. The court shall determine de novo whether the
  requirement conflicts with or is preempted by this chapter. Filing
  an action does not stay the determination unless the court orders
  otherwise. Sovereign and governmental immunity are waived for an
  action authorized by this subsection, but immunity from damages is
  not waived.
  SUBCHAPTER B. LICENSING
         Sec. 437B.051.  HOME LICENSING AUTHORITY. (a)  A mobile food
  vendor shall apply for and maintain each reciprocal mobile food
  vendor license through the vendor's home licensing authority, as
  determined under rules adopted under this chapter.
         (b)  The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  criteria and procedures for designating and changing a home
  licensing authority, resolving disputes, transferring records, and
  ensuring continuity of existing licenses and pending applications.
  The criteria must give priority, in the following order, to the
  vendor's primary commissary or central preparation facility in this
  state, primary vehicle storage location in this state, and
  principal place of business in this state. A vendor may not
  designate or change a location primarily to avoid inspection,
  enforcement, or applicable fees. Unless the department approves
  otherwise, one home licensing authority shall administer all
  licenses held by the vendor.
         (c)  If a home licensing authority cannot otherwise be
  determined or no authorized local health department serves the
  applicable location, the department shall serve as the home
  licensing authority or designate a willing and able local health
  department. A dispute or reassignment may not prevent or materially
  delay an applicant from applying for or obtaining a license. A
  reassignment does not invalidate an existing license or require a
  new application, additional fee, or duplicative inspection solely
  because of the reassignment.
         Sec. 437B.052.  APPLICATION; ISSUANCE; TERM.  (a)  A mobile
  food vendor shall apply through the vendor's home licensing
  authority in the manner prescribed by rules adopted under this
  chapter. The rules must establish reasonable deadlines for
  determining whether an application is complete, conducting any
  required inspection, and issuing a license or notice of proposed
  denial.
         (b)  The home licensing authority shall issue a separate
  license for each food vending vehicle for which the applicant
  submits a complete application, pays the applicable fees, and
  satisfies the applicable licensing and inspection requirements.
         (c)  A reciprocal mobile food vendor license expires on the
  first anniversary of its issuance and may be suspended or revoked as
  provided by this chapter.
         Sec. 437B.053.  PROVISIONAL LICENSE; RENEWAL;
  REINSTATEMENT.  (a)  The executive commissioner by rule shall
  establish requirements and procedures for issuing and
  administering provisional reciprocal mobile food vendor licenses.
  The rules must provide for issuance to an applicant who has
  submitted a complete application and paid the applicable fees if
  the operation qualifies as low risk or the home licensing authority
  fails to act on the application or conduct a required inspection
  within the applicable deadline prescribed by rule under Section
  437B.052. A provisional license may not be issued if the department
  or home licensing authority determines that provisional operation
  would present a material risk to public health or safety. A
  provisional license is valid statewide.
         (b)  A license holder shall renew a license annually through
  the home licensing authority in the manner prescribed by rules
  adopted under this chapter. A timely renewal application for which
  the applicable fee is timely paid continues the existing license
  until the renewed license is issued or the application is
  withdrawn, finally denied, or finally determined to be abandoned.
  Continued licensure under this subsection does not authorize
  operation while the license is suspended or revoked or operation is
  prohibited by an emergency order. The rules must allow a renewal
  applicant to attest that previously submitted information remains
  accurate and submit only information that has changed, expired, or
  is otherwise required by rule.
         (c)  Rules governing abandonment must require written notice
  of any deficiency and a reasonable opportunity to cure. A
  determination of abandonment is subject to Section 437B.201 in the
  same manner as a denial of a renewal application.
         (d)  The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  requirements and procedures for reinstating an expired license. The
  rules must permit reinstatement within a reasonable period after
  expiration. A reinstatement application must be treated as a
  renewal, and a prelicensing inspection may not be required solely
  because the license expired. An expired license does not authorize
  operation before reinstatement.
         Sec. 437B.054.  VEHICLE REPLACEMENT; MATERIAL CHANGES.  (a)  
  A reciprocal mobile food vendor license may not be transferred to
  another person.
         (b)  A license holder may substitute a replacement food
  vending vehicle or materially change the licensed operation in the
  manner prescribed by rules adopted under this chapter. The rules
  may require notice, inspection, reclassification, or amendment or
  reissuance of the license, but may require inspection of a
  replacement vehicle before operation only if the replacement
  vehicle or proposed operation presents a material health risk.
         (c)  The rules must establish reasonable decision deadlines,
  require written notice of determinations, and provide expedited
  department review of a home licensing authority's failure to timely
  act or of a determination made by the authority. The rules must
  provide procedures for temporary operation pending inspection or
  review. Those procedures must authorize temporary operation if the
  department fails to act within the applicable review deadline
  unless the department timely determines in writing, based on
  specific facts, that operation would present a material risk to
  public health or safety. A temporary operating authorization is
  valid statewide, and the home licensing authority shall comply with
  the department's review decision.
         (d)  When operation of a replacement vehicle begins, the
  vehicle previously identified on the license is no longer
  authorized under that license. An amendment or reissuance under
  this section does not change the license's expiration date.
  SUBCHAPTER C. FEES
         Sec. 437B.075.  LICENSE AND RELATED FEE CAPS. (a)  Except as
  provided by Section 437B.076, a fee in connection with an
  application for, issuance, renewal, or administration of a
  reciprocal mobile food vendor license may be imposed only by the
  home licensing authority. For the initial license term and each
  renewal term, the total fees charged by or on behalf of any
  governmental entity in connection with the application, issuance,
  renewal, administration, or related services, including
  application, processing, inspection, technology, database, vehicle
  substitution, material change, amendment, or reissuance fees, may
  not exceed the lesser of the reasonable cost of administering the
  license and providing the related services or:
               (1)  $100 for a Type I food vending vehicle;
               (2)  $200 for a Type II food vending vehicle; or
               (3)  $300 for a Type III food vending vehicle.
         (b)  The caps do not include a separately disclosed
  third-party payment-processing charge if a payment method is
  available without the charge.
         (c)  Not more frequently than once every five years, the
  executive commissioner by rule may increase a fee cap prescribed by
  this subchapter or Section 437B.251(c) by a percentage not
  exceeding the cumulative percentage increase, if any, in the
  Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, U.S. city average,
  all items, published by the United States Bureau of Labor
  Statistics, since the effective date of the cap or, if the cap has
  previously been increased, the date of the most recent increase.
         Sec. 437B.076.  ADDITIONAL INSPECTION FEES. (a)  In
  addition to the amount authorized by Section 437B.075, a home
  licensing authority or local health department may charge a fee for
  an additional inspection or reinspection only as authorized by
  rules adopted under this chapter. The rules must limit the fee to:
               (1)  a reinspection reasonably necessary to verify
  correction of a documented violation;
               (2)  an inspection that documents a material
  food-safety violation and is based on a health complaint, suspected
  foodborne illness, reasonable suspicion of a food-safety
  violation, or an imminent threat to public health or safety; or
               (3)  an inspection reasonably required because of a
  replacement vehicle or material change in the licensed operation. A
  routine, renewal, or prelicensing inspection included under
  Section 437B.075 may not be charged separately, and an additional
  fee may not be charged solely because an inspection is conducted by
  an authority other than the home licensing authority.
         (b)  The fee for each inspection may not exceed the lesser of
  the reasonable cost of conducting the inspection or:
               (1)  $150 for a Type I food vending vehicle;
               (2)  $200 for a Type II food vending vehicle; or
               (3)  $250 for a Type III food vending vehicle.
         (c)  An inspection fee may be retained only if the inspection
  is conducted. The rules must prescribe a reasonable period for
  conducting an inspection for which a fee is collected in advance. If
  the inspection is not conducted within that period, the fee must be
  refunded or, at the vendor's election, credited toward a future
  fee.
         Sec. 437B.077.  FEE TRANSPARENCY; DATABASE REMITTANCE.  (a)  
  An entity that charges a fee under this subchapter shall publish its
  fee schedule and the reasonable-cost basis for each fee before
  collecting the fee, in the manner prescribed by rules adopted under
  this chapter. The rules must require disclosure of any amount
  attributable to an inspection and any amount required to be
  remitted under this section.
         (b)  The executive commissioner by rule shall establish the
  portion of each initial license fee, renewal fee, or separately
  charged inspection fee that must be remitted to the department to
  support the statewide mobile food vendor database. The amount
  remitted from each fee may not exceed the lesser of 10 percent of
  the fee or $25 and must be included within the applicable fee cap.
         (c)  Money remitted under this section may be used, subject
  to legislative appropriation, only for the statewide database and
  related reporting systems.
         (d)  A governmental entity or a contractor acting on its
  behalf may not divide, reclassify, or relabel a fee for the purpose
  or with the effect of avoiding a limitation imposed by this chapter.
         (e)  A fee may not be charged to correct an error
  attributable to a governmental entity or its contractor, correct a
  misspelling or update contact information if the correction or
  update does not require substantive review, or provide an
  electronic replacement copy of a document issued under this
  chapter.
  SUBCHAPTER D. INSPECTIONS AND REPORTING
         Sec. 437B.101.  INSPECTIONS. (a)  A home licensing
  authority shall conduct or arrange routine inspections as
  prescribed by rules adopted under this chapter. The rules must
  establish risk-based inspection frequencies.
         (b)  A local health department other than the home licensing
  authority may conduct a routine inspection only as assigned or
  approved by the department or home licensing authority. The
  department or a local health department may conduct a
  complaint-based or emergency inspection in response to a documented
  health complaint, reasonable suspicion of a violation of statewide
  health standards, suspected foodborne illness, or an imminent
  threat to public health or safety. An inspection under this
  subsection may not be used as a substitute for a routine local
  inspection or permitting program prohibited by this chapter.
         (c)  An inspecting authority shall provide the vendor a copy
  of the completed inspection report and report the results to the
  home licensing authority and the statewide database in the manner
  prescribed by rules adopted under this chapter. Unless otherwise
  expressly authorized by this chapter, a local health department
  that is not the home licensing authority shall refer any
  recommended license or administrative enforcement action to the
  home licensing authority and the department.
         Sec. 437B.102.  EMERGENCY CLOSURE; EMERGENCY SUSPENSION.  
  (a)  A local health department may immediately close a mobile food
  vendor operation within its jurisdiction if it documents facts
  demonstrating that the operation presents an imminent threat to
  public health or safety. A home licensing authority may immediately
  suspend a license statewide if it has reasonable cause to believe
  that continued operation of the licensed vehicle would present an
  imminent threat to public health or safety. The department may do so
  if the home licensing authority fails to act promptly or immediate
  statewide action is necessary. An action under this subsection must
  be limited to what is reasonably necessary to address the threat.
         (b)  A local closure applies only within the issuing
  authority's jurisdiction and does not otherwise affect the
  statewide validity of the license.
         (c)  The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  procedures that require prompt written notice to the vendor and
  appropriate authorities of the basis and scope of an emergency
  action and any known corrective action necessary to lift it,
  provide the vendor a right to an expedited hearing, require prompt
  review or reinspection following corrective action, and provide for
  department review of local action or inaction. The rules must
  prescribe reasonable deadlines and provide that a failure to hold a
  hearing or issue a decision within the applicable deadline is
  considered a final decision for purposes of Subsection (d). An
  emergency action remains effective unless lifted, stayed, or
  reversed. The action must be promptly lifted when the imminent
  threat has been corrected.
         (d)  A person aggrieved by a final decision under this
  section may seek declaratory or injunctive relief in a district
  court in Travis County or a county in which the closure was issued
  or the home licensing authority is located. Sovereign and
  governmental immunity are waived for an action authorized by this
  subsection, but immunity from damages is not waived.
         Sec. 437B.103.  STATEWIDE MOBILE FOOD VENDOR DATABASE. (a)  
  The department shall establish and maintain a statewide mobile food
  vendor database for the administration and enforcement of this
  chapter.
         (b)  The executive commissioner by rule shall prescribe the
  database's contents and its reporting, access, security,
  confidentiality, and correction procedures. The rules must require
  the database to include the licensing, classification, inspection,
  compliance, and enforcement information necessary to implement
  this chapter. A home licensing authority or local health department
  shall submit information as required by rules adopted under this
  chapter. The rules must permit an applicant or license holder to
  review and request correction of information concerning that person
  or the person's food vending vehicle.
         (c)  The department shall make information necessary to
  administer and enforce this chapter available to local health
  departments and provide a public electronic method for verifying
  the current status and, as applicable, classification, expiration
  date, and home licensing authority of a license or other operating
  authorization and the existence and scope of any active closure or
  suspension.
         (d)  The department shall publish and periodically update a
  plain-language guide to the requirements, procedures, rights, and
  protections established by this chapter.
  SUBCHAPTER E. ENFORCEMENT; DUE PROCESS
         Sec. 437B.201.  DENIAL, SUSPENSION, OR REVOCATION.  (a)  A
  home licensing authority may deny an application for, suspend, or
  revoke a reciprocal mobile food vendor license for failure to
  satisfy a material licensing requirement, a material violation of
  this chapter or an applicable rule or statewide health standard,
  fraud or material misrepresentation in connection with the license,
  or operation presenting a material risk to public health or safety.
  An action may not be based solely on a corrected minor technical
  violation that did not create a material health or safety risk. The
  action may affect only an application, license, or vehicle to which
  the grounds for the action apply.
         (b)  Before taking a nonemergency action under this section,
  the home licensing authority shall provide written notice of the
  factual and legal basis for the proposed action and an opportunity
  for a hearing. A timely hearing request stays a nonemergency
  suspension, revocation, or denial of a renewal application until a
  final administrative decision, including disposition of any timely
  appeal to the department.
         (c)  The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  hearing procedures and procedures for appealing a local home
  licensing authority's decision to the department. A proceeding
  before the department under this section is a contested case under
  Chapter 2001, Government Code.
         (d)  A person aggrieved by a final department decision is
  entitled to judicial review under Chapter 2001, Government Code, in
  a district court in Travis County or the county in which the local
  home licensing authority is located. Sovereign and governmental
  immunity are waived to the extent necessary to permit that review,
  but immunity from damages is not waived.
         (e)  This section applies to a provisional reciprocal mobile
  food vendor license.
         Sec. 437B.202.  ADMINISTRATIVE PENALTY. (a)  A person is
  subject to an administrative penalty if the person:
               (1)  operates as a mobile food vendor without a valid
  license or other operating authorization required by this chapter;
               (2)  knowingly violates an emergency closure order;
               (3)  knowingly provides materially false information
  or intentionally omits required material information in connection
  with licensing or enforcement under this chapter; or
               (4)  knowingly prevents or obstructs an inspection
  authorized by this chapter.
         (b)  A penalty may not exceed $500 for each violation.
  Unlicensed operation or violation of an emergency closure order may
  be treated as a separate violation for each day the conduct
  continues.
         (c)  The department or a home licensing authority may assess
  a penalty under this section. A local health department that is not
  the home licensing authority may assess a penalty only for
  unlicensed operation within its jurisdiction, violation of an
  emergency closure order it issued, or obstruction of a
  complaint-based or emergency inspection it conducted. Penalties
  must be assessed as prescribed by rules adopted under this chapter,
  and more than one authority may not assess a penalty for the same
  conduct or the same day of a continuing violation. The penalty
  amount must reflect the seriousness of the violation and threat to
  public health or safety, the person's culpability and compliance
  history, any good-faith corrective action, and the amount necessary
  to deter future violations.
         (d)  Before assessing a penalty, the assessing authority
  shall provide written notice of the alleged violation, proposed
  penalty, factual and legal basis for the action, and an opportunity
  for a hearing. The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  assessment and hearing procedures. A department proceeding is a
  contested case subject to judicial review under Chapter 2001,
  Government Code.
         (e)  A person aggrieved by a final local penalty order may,
  not later than the 30th day after receiving notice of the order,
  file suit for a trial de novo in a district court in the county in
  which the violation occurred or the assessing authority is located.  
  Governmental immunity is waived to permit the suit, but immunity
  from damages is not waived.
         (f)  A penalty may not be collected until the order is final
  and the deadline for seeking judicial review or filing suit under
  Subsection (e) has expired without a proceeding being filed or, if a
  proceeding is timely filed, the proceeding has been finally
  resolved. A local health department may retain a penalty it
  collects to administer this chapter.
  SUBCHAPTER F. TEMPORARY FOOD EVENT PERMITS
         Sec. 437B.251.  TEMPORARY FOOD EVENT PERMITS. (a)  Except as
  otherwise provided by this section, a person operating solely
  within the scope of a valid temporary food event permit is not
  required to hold a reciprocal mobile food vendor license. A
  temporary food event permit authorizes operation only for the
  event, location, period, and food operation covered by the permit.
  A license holder may operate the licensed food vending vehicle at a
  temporary food event without obtaining or paying for a separate
  temporary food event permit or undergoing a routine inspection
  solely because of participation in the event, provided that the
  vendor operates within the scope of the license and complies with
  applicable event-specific requirements not preempted by this
  chapter.
         (b)  A local health department authorized under Chapter 437
  may issue temporary food event permits within its jurisdiction. The
  department shall issue permits where no applicable local permitting
  program exists. The executive commissioner by rule shall establish
  permit requirements and procedures, including permit types and
  durations, risk-based application and inspection requirements,
  reasonable processing deadlines, and criteria for determining when
  temporary food event operations constitute an ongoing mobile food
  business subject to reciprocal licensing. Participation in
  multiple temporary food events alone does not constitute an ongoing
  mobile food business.
         (c)  The total fees charged in connection with an application
  for or issuance of a temporary food event permit, including any
  application, processing, issuance, technology, or inspection fee,
  may not exceed the lesser of the reasonable cost of processing the
  application, issuing the permit, and conducting any included
  inspection or:
               (1)  $50 for a single-event permit valid for not more
  than 14 consecutive days; or
               (2)  $200 for a multiple-event permit valid for the
  period prescribed by rules adopted under this chapter.  The issuing
  authority shall publish its fee schedule before collecting a fee in
  the manner prescribed by rules adopted under this chapter.
         (d)  This section does not limit an event organizer's or
  property owner's control over participation in an event or
  event-wide regulation or inspection of sanitation or shared
  facilities, including a reasonable cost-based fee charged to the
  event organizer.
         SECTION 3.  Chapter 437, Health and Safety Code, is amended
  by adding Section 437.00741 to read as follows:
         Sec. 437.00741.  APPLICATION OF CERTAIN MOBILE FOOD UNIT
  REQUIREMENTS. Sections 437.0073 and 437.0074 do not apply to the
  operation of a mobile food unit if the operation is authorized under

         SECTION 4.  Section 102A.002(3-a), Civil Practice and
  Remedies Code, is amended to read as follows:
               (3-a)  Chapter 437, 437B[437A], or 438, Health and
  Safety Code;
         SECTION 5.  Chapter 437A, Health and Safety Code, is
  repealed.
         SECTION 6.  TRANSITION; IMPLEMENTATION.  (a)  In this
  section:
               (1)  "Conversion date" means June 1, 2028.
               (2)  "Department" means the Department of State Health
  Services.
               (3)  "Executive commissioner" means the executive
  commissioner of the Health and Human Services Commission.
               (4)  "Former Chapter 437B" means Chapter 437B, Health
  and Safety Code, as it existed immediately before amendment by this
  Act.
         (b)  Except as provided by this section, Chapter 437B, Health
  and Safety Code, as amended by this Act, applies beginning on the
  conversion date. Former Chapter 437B governs licensing,
  inspections, and enforcement before that date.
         (c)  A department-issued mobile food vendor license valid or
  continued by law immediately before the conversion date is treated
  on and after that date as a reciprocal mobile food vendor license.
  Unless earlier suspended, revoked, or replaced, a converted license
  remains valid until its existing expiration date, except that:
               (1)  if a timely renewal application is pending
  immediately before the conversion date, the license remains valid
  until a replacement reciprocal mobile food vendor license is issued
  or the application is withdrawn, finally denied, or finally
  determined to be abandoned; and
               (2)  notwithstanding Subdivision (1), a
  department-issued license that expires on or after the effective
  date of this section and before the conversion date is continued
  without an additional fee until the first anniversary of the date
  the license would otherwise have expired.
         (d)  A local mobile food unit permit or substantially
  equivalent health permit valid immediately before the conversion
  date continues to authorize operation within the issuing
  jurisdiction until its expiration, suspension, or revocation,
  issuance of a reciprocal mobile food vendor license for the
  vehicle, or June 1, 2029, whichever occurs first. The issuing
  authority may administer and enforce the permit under the law in
  effect before the conversion date.
         (e)  Not later than February 1, 2028, the executive
  commissioner shall adopt the rules necessary to implement Chapter
  437B, Health and Safety Code, as amended by this Act, and this
  section, including rules governing the designation of home
  licensing authorities; the treatment and transfer of licenses,
  pending applications, and records; and database reporting. The
  rules must provide that an application for issuance or renewal
  pending immediately before the conversion date is treated as an
  application under amended Chapter 4376B and that substantially
  equivalent information, inspections, and fees are accepted or
  credited. Not later than the conversion date, the department shall
  designate a home licensing authority for each converted license
  and, unless the department will serve as that authority, transfer
  to the designated authority the records necessary to administer
  converted licenses and pending applications. Not later than that
  date, the department shall make the statewide database or an
  interim electronic reporting process available with active
  licensing, inspection, compliance, and enforcement records.
  Notwithstanding Section 437B.052(c), Health and Safety Code, as
  amended by this Act, the rules may assign an initial term of six to
  18 months to a license issued in connection with the transition,
  other than a license converted under Subsection (c), and must
  provide for proration of any time-based portion of the applicable
  fee. The department and home licensing authorities may accept and
  process applications before the conversion date, but a license may
  not take effect and a substantive requirement may not be imposed
  before that date.
         (f)  Chapter 437B, Health and Safety Code, as amended by this
  Act, applies to a local requirement regardless of when the
  requirement was adopted.
         (g)  A violation of former Chapter 437B or Chapter 437A,
  Health and Safety Code, occurring before the conversion date is
  governed by the law in effect when the violation occurred. That law
  continues in effect for investigating, adjudicating, and enforcing
  the violation and for completing a proceeding pending immediately
  before the conversion date.
         SECTION 7.  EFFECTIVE DATE. Section 6 of this Act and this
  section take effect immediately if this Act receives a vote of
  two-thirds of all the members elected to each house, as provided by
  Section 39, Article III, Texas Constitution. If this Act does not
  receive the vote necessary for immediate effect, Section 6 of this
  Act and this section take effect September 1, 2027. The remainder of
  this Act takes effect June 1, 2028.
