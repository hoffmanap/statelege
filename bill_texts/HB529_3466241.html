By: Bhojani H.B. No. 529
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to water conservation plans, data center and related
  facility water use and wastewater reporting, water resource impact
  assessments, notice to local entities, public participation, and
  public disclosure; providing for enforcement and fees.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Subchapter K, Chapter 16, Water Code, is amended
  by adding Sections 16.405, 16.406, 16.407, 16.408, 16.409, 16.410,
  16.411, 16.412, and 16.413 to read as follows:
         Sec. 16.405.  DEFINITIONS. In Sections 16.405 through
  16.413:
               (1)  "Commercial use" means the use of water by a place
  of business, such as a hotel, restaurant, or office building. The
  term does not include multifamily residential, agricultural,
  industrial, or institutional use. 
               (2)  "Covered data center" means a facility or
  integrated campus in this state used primarily to house or operate
  equipment for digital computing, processing, storage,
  transmission, or validation, including equipment for
  cryptocurrency or digital asset mining, together with related
  facilities, if the facility or campus: 
                     (A)  has, or is reasonably projected to have, an
  aggregate electric demand of at least 75 megawatts, including
  electricity generated on the site or supplied without an
  interconnection to a transmission or distribution system; or
                     (B)  withdraws, purchases, receives, or otherwise
  obtains, or is reasonably projected to obtain, at least 300,000
  gallons of water for the operations of the facility, campus, and
  related facilities during any consecutive 30-day period. 
               (3)  "Operator" means the person with operational
  control over the water supply, cooling, or water management systems
  of a covered data center. If more than one person has operational
  control, the persons shall designate an operator responsible for
  compliance with Sections 16.405 through 16.413. 
               (4)  "Material expansion" means a change to a covered
  data center that increases the maximum monthly water demand stated
  in the most recent assessment submitted under Section 16.407 by at
  least 25 percent, adds a materially different source of water
  supply, or materially changes the destination or capacity required
  for wastewater service.
               (5)  "Water consumed" means water that is not returned
  to a water source, water supplier, or wastewater system after use,
  including water lost through evaporation, as determined under
  commission rules.
               (6)  "Water returned" means water discharged after use
  to a surface water body, groundwater resource, water supplier, or
  wastewater system, including a quantity conveyed for treatment or
  authorized reuse.
               (7)  "Water withdrawn" means water taken directly from
  a surface water or groundwater resource, other than water obtained
  from a public or private water supplier. 
               (8)  "Affected local recipient" means: 
                     (A)  each county in which a covered data center is
  located and each county contiguous to that county; 
                     (B)  each municipality whose corporate limits or
  extraterritorial jurisdiction includes any portion of the tract
  containing a covered data center, and each municipality whose
  corporate limits are within one mile of that tract; 
                     (C)  each groundwater conservation district with
  jurisdiction over a withdrawal or well supplying a covered data
  center; and
                     (D)  each political subdivision, water supply
  corporation, or public or private water or wastewater service
  provider that provides service to a covered data center or whose
  legally established service area includes all or part of the tract
  containing the facility. 
                     (9)  "Related facility" means a cooling
  installation, water withdrawal, treatment, storage, or reuse
  facility, wastewater management installation, dedicated power
  generation or energy storage installation, or other accessory
  infrastructure that directly supports a facility described by
  Subdivision (2) and is located on contiguous or adjacent property
  or operated as part of the same integrated development, regardless
  of whether operated through a separate legal entity. 
         Sec. 16.406.  APPLICABILITY; REGISTRATION; COMMISSION
  AUTHORITY.
         (a)  The commission shall administer and enforce Sections
  16.405 through 16.413 and shall establish and maintain a
  registration program for covered data centers. 
         (b)  The operator of a proposed covered data center shall
  register the facility with the commission not later than the 120th
  day before the date the facility is expected to begin operation. 
         (c)  The operator of a facility that first meets the
  definition of a covered data center after beginning operation shall
  register the facility not later than the 90th day after the date the
  operator knows or reasonably should know that the facility meets
  that definition.
         (d)  For purposes of determining whether the thresholds
  under Section 16.405(2) are met and of reporting water use, the
  commission shall treat a data processing or storage facility and
  its related facilities as one covered data center when they are part
  of a single integrated development or share dedicated water supply,
  wastewater, or cooling infrastructure. This requirement applies
  regardless of separate legal entities, water providers, or on-site
  electric generation. A project may not be divided into separate
  facilities or accounts to evade this subchapter. 
         (e)  Sections 16.405 through 16.413 apply without regard to
  whether a covered data center obtains water from a municipal
  utility, a district, a water supply corporation, a private
  provider, or its own groundwater or surface water source, or
  whether the water is classified as commercial, industrial,
  municipal, or another type of use. The use of electricity generated
  on-site or outside the ERCOT region does not exempt a facility that
  otherwise meets the definition of a covered data center.
         (f)  A registration must identify the name and location of
  the covered data center and each related facility included in the
  reporting boundary; the operator and a responsible contact person;
  the anticipated or actual date of operation; the basis for coverage
  under Section 16.405(2); the known sources of water supply and
  wastewater service; and the affected local recipients identified
  under Section 16.405(8). The operator shall update registration
  information not later than the 30th day after a material change. 
         (g)  Not later than the 10th business day after submitting a
  registration, water resource impact assessment, facility water
  conservation plan, annual report, or material amendment to the
  commission, the operator shall send a copy of the corresponding
  nonconfidential submission to each affected local recipient by
  electronic means or other documented delivery method approved by
  rule. The operator shall certify delivery to the commission and
  shall bear the cost of preparing and transmitting the copies. 
         (h)  The commission shall maintain a current electronic list
  of affected local recipients for each facility and shall notify
  those recipients of public hearing and comment opportunities under
  Section 16.410. This section does not require an affected local
  recipient to maintain a website or create an independent filing
  system. 
         Sec. 16.407.  WATER RESOURCE IMPACT ASSESSMENT.
         (a)  An operator required to register under Section 16.406
  shall submit to the commission a water resource impact assessment
  with the registration. An operator proposing a material expansion
  shall submit an updated assessment not later than the 120th day
  before the expected date of the expansion. 
         (b)  The assessment must contain, to the extent reasonably
  ascertainable, the following information: 
               (1)  the current or proposed source of each water
  supply, identifying groundwater, surface water, purchased potable
  water, reclaimed water, and other sources separately; 
               (2)  the name of each water and wastewater service
  provider, if any; the groundwater conservation district, if any;
  and the applicable river basin, aquifer, or other water resource
  identified by the operator; 
               (3)  the location of each known or proposed withdrawal
  and return point, including latitude and longitude when reasonably
  available; 
               (4)  the projected average and maximum monthly
  quantities of water withdrawn or received, consumed, returned, and
  reused, including demand attributable to related facilities,
  seasonal conditions, and reasonably anticipated drought
  conditions; 
               (5)  the water cooling and management technologies to
  be used, the assumptions and methodology supporting the
  projections, and reasonably available alternatives for reducing
  potable water use;
               (6)  a description of reasonably foreseeable water
  quantity and water quality effects associated with the proposed
  use, including expected temperature changes or pollutants in water
  returns, anticipated wastewater discharges, and the need for
  permits governing a discharge; 
               (7)  a discussion, based on reasonably available public
  information and information provided by the operator's water and
  wastewater service providers, of water supply availability,
  existing and projected local demands, water and wastewater service
  capacity, and the compatibility of the proposed use with applicable
  drought contingency and water conservation plans;
               (8)  to the extent reasonably ascertainable, potential
  cumulative effects of the proposed use with existing covered data
  center water demands in the county, regional water planning area,
  and shared water source;
               (9)  identification of applicable water rights,
  groundwater district authorizations, water supply and wastewater
  service agreements, and discharge authorizations, if any, without
  requiring submission of confidential contractual terms; 
               (10)  projected average and maximum monthly wastewater
  volumes, the expected type and destination of the wastewater
  discharge or transfer, the receiving wastewater service provider or
  treatment facility if known, and any reasonably foreseeable effect
  on wastewater service capacity; and
               (11)  to the extent reasonably ascertainable, an
  explanation of how shared or related facilities have been included
  in the water use and wastewater projections, with measures to
  prevent double counting. 
         (c)  The assessment must identify data limitations and
  distinguish measurements from estimates. The commission shall
  prescribe reasonable methodologies and may request supplemental
  information reasonably necessary to determine whether an
  assessment is complete and accurate. 
         (d)  An assessment under this section is for information,
  conservation planning, and public disclosure and is not a permit,
  water right, or agency determination that a proposed water use is
  authorized or prohibited. Submission of an assessment does not
  substitute for any authorization otherwise required by law. 
         Sec. 16.408.  FACILITY WATER CONSERVATION PLAN.
         (a)  An operator shall submit with the water resource impact
  assessment required by Section 16.407 a facility water conservation
  plan that describes the practices, techniques, and technologies the
  operator proposes to use to reduce waste and improve efficient
  water use. 
         (b)  The plan must include: 
               (1)  a description of water measurement, leak
  detection, inspection, and maintenance practices; 
               (2)  an evaluation of reasonably available water reuse,
  recycling, and alternative nonpotable water sources, including an
  explanation when an alternative is not reasonably practicable; 
               (3)  measurable, facility-specific water efficiency
  goals, a schedule for implementing the measures selected by the
  operator, and the method of measuring progress toward those goals; 
               (4)  procedures for coordinating with the applicable
  water or wastewater service provider, including the provider's
  drought contingency plan, and complying with restrictions
  otherwise lawfully imposed under existing law or a service
  agreement; 
               (5)  practices for coordinating with wastewater
  service providers regarding discharge volumes, pretreatment
  requirements, and service capacity, to the extent otherwise
  required by an applicable law, permit, or agreement. 
         (c)  An operator shall implement the measures identified as
  commitments in the operator's plan and shall review the plan at
  least once every five years and after each material expansion.
  Failure to achieve a projected efficiency goal, without more, does
  not constitute a violation if the operator implemented the
  committed measures and accurately reported the results. 
         (d)  The commission may prescribe minimum plan content and a
  form for submission but may not under this section impose a water
  withdrawal quota or require a specific cooling technology. 
         Sec. 16.409.  ANNUAL WATER USE REPORTING; MEASUREMENT. 
         (a)  Not later than March 31 of each year, the operator of
  each covered data center shall submit to the commission a report
  describing the facility's water use for the preceding calendar
  year. 
         (b)  The annual report must state separately, for each month
  and each source or provider to the extent reasonably practicable,
  the quantities attributable to the covered data center and its
  related facilities, without duplicating transfers within the
  reporting boundary:
               (1)  the actual volume of water withdrawn directly from
  surface water and groundwater resources; 
               (2)  the actual volume of water purchased or received
  from each municipal, district, nonprofit, or private water
  supplier; 
               (3)  the volume of water consumed, returned, and reused
  at the facility, including the destination of returned water and a
  separate identification of water conveyed to a wastewater service
  provider; 
               (4)  the maximum quantity of water used during any
  single day of the month, if measured or reasonably estimable; 
               (5)  whether each reported quantity is metered,
  calculated from billing records, or estimated by a generally
  accepted method prescribed by the commission; 
               (6)  a summary of water conservation measures
  implemented, progress toward the plan's efficiency goals, and
  material changes in water or wastewater service sources or facility
  operations; and
               (7)  the volume of wastewater conveyed to each
  wastewater service provider or other authorized receiving
  facility, and the basis for measurement or estimation, without
  requiring disclosure of information that is confidential under
  other law.
         (c)  An operator shall use metered measurements or supplier
  billing data if reasonably available. If reliable direct
  measurement is not reasonably available, the operator shall use a
  scientifically reasonable estimate or indirect calculation
  approved by commission rule and disclose the method and its
  limitations.
         (d)  The commission shall establish reasonable measurement
  and reporting standards appropriate to different facility types.
  The commission may require metering of new water supply and return
  connections when practicable, and shall provide reasonable
  compliance periods for existing facilities. 
         (e)  This section applies to water purchased or received from
  any public or private water supplier, including a municipal
  utility, district, or water supply corporation, even if the
  supplier separately reports its withdrawals. The commission shall
  identify purchased or transferred water separately in data provided
  to the board to avoid double counting statewide or regional
  withdrawals. 
         (f)  Notwithstanding Section 16.404, the commission may
  require facility-specific information under this section that is
  more detailed than a water supplier's billing system can produce,
  if the information can reasonably be obtained by the operator
  through metering or an accepted estimation method. 
         Sec. 16.410.  PUBLIC DISCLOSURE; PUBLIC HEARING AND COMMENT. 
         (a)  The commission shall establish and maintain a publicly
  accessible Internet website containing a registry of covered data
  centers and nonconfidential information submitted or prepared
  under Sections 16.406 through 16.409. The commission, and not a
  county, municipality, district, or water supply corporation, is
  responsible under this subchapter for maintaining the statewide
  project website.
         (b)  For each covered data center, the website must identify
  the facility, operator, county and regional water planning area,
  water source categories, and water and wastewater service provider
  names, if any, and must make available: 
               (1)  the nonconfidential water resource impact
  assessment and facility water conservation plan; 
               (2)  annual facility-level data on water withdrawn,
  received, consumed, returned, and reused and wastewater conveyed
  for service, including monthly totals and the water source
  categories from which water was obtained; and
               (3)  any public notice, written comment, hearing
  summary, and commission response prepared under this section. 
         (b-1)  The operator shall furnish the commission with
  project-specific narrative, maps, notices, and nonconfidential
  electronic documents needed for the website in a format approved by
  the commission. The commission may not require an affected local
  recipient to establish or maintain a project website to satisfy
  this section.
         (c)  For an initial assessment submitted before operation of
  a proposed covered data center, or an updated assessment submitted
  before a material expansion, the commission shall conduct at least
  one public informational hearing in the county in which the
  facility is or will be located. The commission may coordinate a
  joint hearing with an affected local governmental entity that
  consents and may provide remote access. 
         (d)  The commission shall provide notice of the hearing at
  least 30 days before the hearing by publishing notice on its website
  and in a newspaper of general circulation in the county and by
  electronically notifying affected local recipients. The notice
  must identify the proposed facility, provide the date, time, and
  place of the hearing, and explain how the public may inspect
  nonconfidential documents and submit comments.
         (e)  The commission shall accept public comments during the
  hearing and for at least 30 days after the hearing, including
  comments submitted electronically by affected local recipients.
  The commission shall prepare a written response addressing
  significant comments concerning water and wastewater service,
  water availability, cumulative effects, conservation measures, and
  the completeness of the assessment. The response shall be posted on
  the website and provided to the operator and affected local
  recipients.
         (f)  A public informational hearing under this section is not
  a contested case hearing and does not constitute an approval or
  denial of a water use or a permit. 
         (g)  The operator shall provide to the commission, in an
  electronic format specified by rule, a complete submission and a
  separate proposed public version of each submission, with each
  proposed redaction identified and supported by a citation to an
  applicable law providing confidentiality. A claim that information
  is proprietary, without more, does not make that information
  confidential. The commission shall make nonconfidential
  information available under this section in accordance with Chapter
  552, Government Code, and other applicable law. 
         (h)  Information made confidential by state or federal law
  must be withheld from public posting to the extent required by that
  law. A designation as proprietary does not independently justify
  withholding the facility name, county, water source categories,
  water or wastewater supplier names, or monthly and annual volume
  data required to be published under Subsection (b). The duties of
  governmental bodies and interested third parties concerning
  requests for information are governed by Chapter 552, Government
  Code, including Section 552.305 of that code. An operator asserting
  a proprietary exception is responsible for submitting the arguments
  and evidence required by applicable law; a county, municipality,
  district, or other local governmental entity is not required by
  this subchapter to advocate the operator's proprietary interest. 
         (i)  The operator shall pay the reasonable direct costs of
  preparing and providing project-specific documents, required
  publication of hearing notices, and other project-specific
  informational materials required by this section. The commission by
  rule may assess a fee not exceeding its reasonable, documented
  direct costs of providing the project-specific hearing, notice, and
  public website posting required by this section, less costs paid
  directly by the operator. Money collected under this subsection
  shall be deposited as provided by Section 5.701 and used only as
  authorized by law. The commission may not assess the same cost
  twice.
         (j)  An affected local governmental entity may, to the extent
  otherwise authorized by law, hold a separate informational hearing
  or participate in a joint hearing with the commission. This
  subchapter does not require an affected local recipient to conduct
  a hearing, make a determination about proprietary claims, or incur
  unreimbursed project-specific publication or website expenses.
         Sec. 16.411.  STATEWIDE ANALYSIS; INTERAGENCY COOPERATION. 
         (a)  The commission shall annually analyze information
  reported under this subchapter to evaluate individual and
  cumulative water demands associated with covered data centers,
  including withdrawals, consumption, returns, reuse, wastewater
  service demands, regional water availability, and reasonably
  ascertainable water quality effects. The analysis must separately
  present nonconfidential aggregated results by county and by
  regional water planning area and, when practicable, by major
  aquifer or river basin.
         (b)  The commission shall coordinate with the board,
  affected groundwater conservation districts, regional water
  planning groups, municipalities, counties, water supply
  corporations, and public or private water and wastewater service
  providers. The commission may request or exchange information to
  the extent authorized by law and shall use reasonably available
  regional water planning and utility service data in its analysis. 
         (c)  Not later than November 1 of each year, the commission
  shall publish a statewide report, including county and regional
  water planning area tables, on the commission's website and
  transmit the report to the governor, the lieutenant governor, the
  speaker of the house of representatives, and the standing
  committees of each house having primary jurisdiction over water
  resources. The commission shall provide the underlying
  nonconfidential data and a county and regional water planning area
  summary to the board and applicable regional water planning groups. 
         (d)  A report under this section must distinguish direct
  withdrawals from water received from suppliers, as well as
  wastewater conveyed to a provider, to avoid double counting and
  shall identify material data limitations, allocation assumptions,
  and instances of information withheld under applicable
  confidentiality law. 
         (e)  The board shall consider information provided under
  this section, including county and regional water planning area
  totals, in preparing the next regularly scheduled update of the
  state water plan under Section 16.051 and shall make the
  information available to regional water planning groups for use in
  preparing regional water plans under Section 16.053. Nothing in
  this subsection requires the board to amend a plan before its next
  regularly scheduled update. 
         Sec. 16.412.  COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT.
         (a)  The commission is the lead state regulatory and
  enforcement agency for Sections 16.405 through 16.413 and rules
  adopted under those sections. This subsection does not displace or
  limit the lawful regulatory, contractual, or enforcement authority
  of a local governmental entity or water or wastewater service
  provider under other law. 
         (b)  The operator shall retain records reasonably necessary
  to substantiate a registration, impact assessment, conservation
  plan, and annual report for at least five years and shall make those
  records available to the commission on lawful request. 
         (c)  The commission may review submissions, require
  correction of materially incomplete or inaccurate information, and
  issue notices of violation and orders requiring timely compliance
  with the reporting, recordkeeping, and plan implementation duties
  imposed by Sections 16.405 through 16.413. 
         (d)  Notwithstanding Section 7.051(b), a violation of
  Sections 16.405 through 16.413 or a rule adopted by the commission
  under those sections is enforceable in the manner provided by
  Chapter 7 for a violation of a provision of this code within the
  commission's jurisdiction or a rule adopted by the commission under
  a provision of this code within the commission's jurisdiction. 
         (e)  The commission may assess administrative penalties and
  obtain other remedies available under Chapter 7, subject to the
  procedures and limitations of that chapter. An enforcement order
  under this section may compel submission, correction,
  verification, or implementation of a measure expressly committed to
  in a filed conservation plan, but may not independently require a
  reduction in a water withdrawal or water use otherwise lawful under
  existing law. 
         Sec. 16.413.  RULEMAKING; CONSTRUCTION OF LAW.
         (a)  The commission shall adopt rules necessary to implement
  and enforce Sections 16.405 through 16.413, including standards for
  registration, identification of affected local recipients, water
  and wastewater measurement, accepted estimates, impact assessment
  content, conservation plans, public notice, operator-supported
  confidentiality claims, electronic reporting, and cost recovery
  under Section 16.410. In adopting rules, the commission shall
  consult with the board and representatives of municipalities,
  counties, groundwater conservation districts, water and wastewater
  service providers, and affected data center operators. 
         (b)  Nothing in Sections 16.405 through 16.413: 
               (1)  creates or modifies a right to appropriate,
  withdraw, or use surface water or groundwater, or a duty of a water
  or wastewater service provider to furnish service, or modifies a
  permit, authorization, contract, or property right otherwise
  recognized by law;
               (2)  authorizes the commission to deny, suspend,
  revoke, reduce, condition, or otherwise alter a person's existing
  lawful water withdrawal or use solely on the basis of an assessment,
  conservation plan, report, or public comment required by those
  sections; 
               (3)  supersedes the authority of a groundwater
  conservation district under Chapter 36; a water right holder under
  Chapter 11; a municipality, county, or district; or a public or
  private water or wastewater service provider under otherwise
  applicable law; or 
               (4)  authorizes the commission to impose a fee for
  water or wastewater infrastructure costs, alter a utility's lawful
  rates or charges, or modify the terms of an existing water or
  wastewater service agreement; the fees expressly authorized by
  Section 16.410 are limited to administration of that section.
         (c)  Nothing in this section affects the commission's
  independent authority under other law concerning water quality,
  discharge permits, or enforcement of existing permits and orders. 
         (d)  This subchapter does not preempt or diminish otherwise
  lawful municipal, county, or district authority over zoning, land
  use, development, building and fire codes, floodplain management,
  stormwater, nuisance abatement, or other local matters; or the
  authority of an affected water or wastewater service provider to
  establish and enforce lawful rates, tariffs, capacity
  requirements, service conditions, conservation or drought
  restrictions, or service agreements. No such authority is newly
  created or expanded by this subsection. 
         (e)  A reporting, hearing, or public disclosure duty created
  by this subchapter is independent of, and does not substitute for,
  any permit, consent, or service authorization required under
  another law. Except for an operator's obligations expressly stated
  in this subchapter, the subchapter does not require a local
  governmental entity or water or wastewater service provider to
  issue a permit, approve a development, or provide service. 
         SECTION 2.  The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality
  shall adopt the rules necessary to implement Sections 16.405
  through 16.413, Water Code, as added by this Act, not later than May
  1, 2028. 
         SECTION 3.  (a) Notwithstanding Section 16.406(c), Water
  Code, as added by this Act, an operator of a covered data center, as
  defined by Section 16.405, Water Code, as added by this Act, that is
  operating before January 1, 2029, and meets that definition on that
  date shall register the facility and submit the initial water
  resource impact assessment and facility water conservation plan
  required by this Act not later than July 1, 2029. 
         (b)  The annual report required by Section 16.409, Water
  Code, as added by this Act, is first due on March 31, 2030, for
  calendar year 2029. 
         (c)  The advance-registration, assessment, and
  public-hearing requirements applicable to a proposed covered data
  center apply to a facility expected to begin operation on or after
  January 1, 2029. A material expansion expected to begin on or after
  that date is subject to the applicable advance-submission
  requirements. 
         (d)  The first statewide report required by Section
  16.411(c), Water Code, as added by this Act, is due November 1,
  2030.
         SECTION 4.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
