By: Mizani H.B. No. 1073
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to restrictions on the acquisition of single-family
  residential property by insurance companies and other
  institutional investors; providing a civil penalty.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Chapter 5, Property Code, is amended by adding
  Subchapter I to read as follows:
  SUBCHAPTER I. ACQUISITION OF SINGLE-FAMILY RESIDENCES BY CERTAIN
  INSTITUTIONAL INVESTORS
         Sec. 5.301.  DEFINITIONS. In this subchapter:
               (1)  "Affiliate" means a person that directly or
  indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control
  with another person. "Control" means the power to direct the
  management, investment decisions, or acquisition or disposition of
  real property of a person through ownership, contract, service as
  investment adviser or manager, or otherwise.
               (2)  "Business entity" means a corporation, limited
  liability company, partnership, real estate investment trust,
  business trust, investment fund, or other for-profit legal entity.
               (3)  "Covered institutional investor" means:
                     (A)  an insurer, insurance holding company, or
  affiliate controlled by an insurer or insurance holding company;
                     (B)  a business entity that, together with its
  affiliates, has consolidated total assets of at least $100 million,
  as shown by financial statements prepared in the ordinary course of
  business for the most recent fiscal year;
                     (C)  a business entity that, alone or together
  with its affiliates, directly or indirectly owns or exercises
  investment control over 100 or more single-family residences in the
  United States; or
                     (D)  an agent, nominee, trust, or other person
  acting on behalf of or for the benefit of a person described by
  Paragraph (A), (B), or (C) in acquiring a single-family residence.
               (4)  "Insurer" means a person authorized or required to
  be authorized under the Insurance Code to engage in the business of
  insurance in this state, or licensed to engage in that business in
  another state.
               (5)  "Investment purpose" means acquiring property
  principally to receive rental income, obtain appreciation in value,
  or resell or assign the property for investment profit. The term
  does not include a purchase principally for occupancy by an
  individual as the individual's residence.
               (6)  "Single-family residence" means real property in
  this state improved with a dwelling containing not more than two
  residential units, including a detached residence, townhouse, or
  separately owned condominium unit. The term does not include a
  manufactured home, a building containing three or more residential
  units, or unimproved land.
         Sec. 5.302.  PROHIBITED ACQUISITIONS. (a) Except as
  provided by Section 5.303, a covered institutional investor may not
  purchase or otherwise acquire, directly or indirectly, an ownership
  or beneficial ownership interest in a single-family residence for
  an investment purpose.
         (b)  The prohibition applies to an acquisition through a
  deed, contract for deed, assignment, option exercised to acquire
  ownership, transfer of a controlling ownership interest in a
  property-owning entity, or any other transaction that gives a
  covered institutional investor investment control of the
  residence.
         (c)  In determining whether a person is a covered
  institutional investor, residences and assets held or controlled by
  affiliates and by entities subject to common investment management
  or acquisition decisions must be aggregated. A person may not
  divide acquisitions among affiliated persons, trusts, or nominees
  to avoid this subchapter.
         (d)  This section applies without regard to the state or
  country in which a covered institutional investor is organized or
  maintains its principal place of business.
         Sec. 5.303.  EXCEPTIONS. (a) Section 5.302 does not
  prohibit:
               (1)  the acquisition of a residence by an individual
  for the individual's own occupancy, or by a revocable trust or
  entity solely for that purpose and wholly owned by the occupying
  individual or members of the individual's family;
               (2)  an acquisition by this state, a political
  subdivision, a housing authority, or a nonprofit organization
  exempt from federal income taxation under Section 501(c)(3),
  Internal Revenue Code of 1986, for an affordable housing or
  homeownership program;
               (3)  the construction of a new residence by a
  developer, the ownership of the residence by the developer pending
  its initial sale, or the initial sale of the residence by the
  developer to an individual for occupancy as a residence;
               (4)  an acquisition through foreclosure, a deed in lieu
  of foreclosure, enforcement of a valid security interest, or
  satisfaction of a debt previously contracted in good faith, if the
  acquiring person does not use the acquisition as a means of
  expanding a residential investment portfolio and offers the
  residence for sale within 24 months after acquiring it;
               (5)  an acquisition of a residence for bona fide
  temporary occupancy by an employee of the purchaser, if the
  residence is not held principally to produce rental income or
  investment appreciation; or
               (6)  a transfer between affiliates of a residence
  lawfully owned before September 1, 2027, if the transfer does not
  result in a new person obtaining ultimate investment control of the
  residence.
         (b)  A district court may extend the 24-month period under
  Subsection (a)(4) for good cause shown, including pending
  litigation, an inability to deliver marketable title, or other
  circumstances beyond the owner's reasonable control.
         (c)  This section does not exempt a purchase prohibited by
  federal law.
         Sec. 5.304.  PURCHASER CERTIFICATION. (a) Before closing
  on the acquisition of a single-family residence, a purchaser that
  is a business entity shall deliver to the seller a written
  certification identifying the purchaser and any person that
  directly or indirectly controls the purchaser and stating either:
               (1)  that the purchaser is not a covered institutional
  investor; or
               (2)  the specific exception under Section 5.303 under
  which the acquisition is permitted.
         (b)  The purchaser shall retain the certification and
  reasonable supporting records for four years after the acquisition
  and provide them to the attorney general on lawful request.
         (c)  A seller, real estate broker, title insurance company,
  escrow agent, or county clerk is not required to investigate the
  accuracy of a certification and is not liable under this subchapter
  solely for participating in a transaction unless the person
  knowingly makes a material false statement to facilitate a
  prohibited acquisition.
         (d)  A county clerk may not refuse to record an otherwise
  recordable instrument solely because a certification under this
  section was not presented to the clerk.
         Sec. 5.305.  ENFORCEMENT; CIVIL PENALTY. (a) The attorney
  general may investigate a suspected violation of this subchapter
  and bring an action in a district court in the county in which the
  residence is located to obtain injunctive relief, a civil penalty,
  or an order under Section 5.306.
         (b)  A covered institutional investor that knowingly
  violates Section 5.302 or knowingly provides a materially false
  certification under Section 5.304 is liable to the state for a civil
  penalty for each affected residence in an amount not to exceed the
  greater of $100,000 or 20 percent of the purchase price of the
  residence.
         (c)  The court may award the attorney general reasonable
  attorney's fees and costs if the attorney general prevails in an
  action under this section. A civil penalty collected under this
  section shall be deposited in the general revenue fund.
         Sec. 5.306.  DIVESTITURE; PROTECTION OF INNOCENT PARTIES.
  (a) On finding that a covered institutional investor acquired a
  residence in violation of Section 5.302, a district court may order
  the investor to convey the residence to a purchaser not prohibited
  from acquiring it under this subchapter within 180 days after the
  date the judgment becomes final.
         (b)  If the investor fails to comply with an order under
  Subsection (a), the court may appoint a receiver to sell the
  residence under court supervision. Sale proceeds remaining after
  payment of reasonable sale expenses, valid liens, and amounts owed
  under a final judgment shall be paid to the owner.
         (c)  A violation of this subchapter does not automatically
  invalidate a deed or impair the rights of an innocent seller,
  tenant, lienholder, or subsequent bona fide purchaser for value
  without notice of the violation. An order under this section is
  subject to applicable federal law and the rights of persons not
  responsible for the violation.
         (d)  An action seeking divestiture of a residence under this
  section must be filed not later than the fourth anniversary of the
  date the prohibited acquisition is recorded in the real property
  records.
         Sec. 5.307.  CONSTRUCTION OF SUBCHAPTER. (a) This
  subchapter does not require the sale or divestiture of a
  single-family residence lawfully acquired before September 1,
  2027, or prohibit the renewal of a lease of that residence.
         (b)  This subchapter does not prohibit an investment in a
  mortgage, mortgage-backed security, or other debt instrument that
  does not convey an ownership or controlling beneficial interest in
  a specific single-family residence, except when the investment is a
  device to evade Section 5.302.
         (c)  This subchapter supplements and does not limit any
  restriction or remedy provided by federal law, including 42 U.S.C.
  Section 19521. Nothing in this subchapter authorizes an
  acquisition prohibited by federal law.
         SECTION 2.  Section 424.064, Insurance Code, is amended by
  adding Subsection (g) to read as follows:
         (g)  Nothing in this section, including Subsection (d),
  authorizes an insurer to acquire a single-family residence for an
  investment purpose in violation of Subchapter I, Chapter 5,
  Property Code.
         SECTION 3.  Section 425.119, Insurance Code, is amended by
  adding Subsection (i) to read as follows:
         (i)  Nothing in this section, including Subsection (f),
  authorizes an insurance company to acquire a single-family
  residence for an investment purpose in violation of Subchapter I,
  Chapter 5, Property Code.
         SECTION 4.  Subchapter I, Chapter 5, Property Code, as added
  by this Act, applies only to a contract to purchase or other
  transaction to acquire an interest in a single-family residence
  entered into on or after the effective date of this Act. A contract
  or transaction entered into before that date is governed by the law
  in effect immediately before the effective date of this Act, and
  that law is continued in effect for that purpose.
         SECTION 5.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
