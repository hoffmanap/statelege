By: Bhojani H.B. No. 547
 
 
 
A BILL TO BE ENTITLED
 
AN ACT
  relating to county noise study requirements and site construction
  approval for facilities operated by certain large load electric
  customers, authorizing a fee.
         BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF TEXAS:
         SECTION 1.  Subchapter B, Chapter 37, Utilities Code, is
  amended by adding Section 37.0562 to read as follows:
         Sec. 37.0562.  COUNTY NOISE STUDIES FOR LARGE LOAD
  CUSTOMERS.  (a)  In this section, "large load customer" means an
  entity described by Section 37.0561(a) that is subject to the
  interconnection standards required under Section 37.0561(c),
  including a successor in interest to the entity.
         (b)  A county may require a large load customer that proposes
  to construct or expand a facility in the unincorporated area of the
  county to prepare, submit, and publicly post pre-construction and
  post-construction noise studies in accordance with Subchapter G,
  Chapter 233, Local Government Code.
         (c)  A county may condition the issuance and continued
  validity of a site construction approval for a facility described
  by Subsection (b) on compliance with the noise study, publication,
  and mitigation requirements established under Subchapter G,
  Chapter 233, Local Government Code.
         (d)  This section does not authorize a county to regulate the
  interconnection or transmission service of an electric utility or
  to prevent compliance with a lawful instruction issued by the
  independent organization certified under Section 39.151 for the
  ERCOT power region during an energy emergency.
         SECTION 2.  Chapter 233, Local Government Code, is amended
  by adding Subchapter G to read as follows:
  SUBCHAPTER G. NOISE STUDIES AND SITE CONSTRUCTION APPROVAL FOR
  LARGE LOAD CUSTOMER FACILITIES
         Sec. 233.171.  DEFINITIONS. In this subchapter:
               (1)  "A-weighted sound level" means a sound level
  expressed in A-weighted decibels, or dB(A), measured using the
  A-weighting network of a sound level meter.
               (2)  "Covered facility" means a building, group of
  buildings, or associated equipment at a single site in the
  unincorporated area of a county that is proposed to be constructed
  or expanded for use by a large load customer in connection with a
  new or expanded interconnection subject to Section 37.0561(c),
  Utilities Code. The term includes exterior cooling, ventilation,
  mechanical, and customer-owned backup generating equipment serving
  the site.
               (3)  "Large load customer" has the meaning assigned by
  Section 37.0562(a), Utilities Code.
               (4)  "Noise study" means an acoustical assessment
  prepared or certified by an engineer licensed to practice
  professional engineering in this state who has experience in
  acoustical measurement or modeling.
               (5)  "Receiving property" means real property other
  than the covered facility site at which sound attributable to the
  covered facility is assessed under this subchapter.
               (6)  "Site construction approval" means the permit or
  other authorization issued by a county under this subchapter before
  construction or expansion of a covered facility may begin.
         Sec. 233.172.  COUNTY AUTHORITY; APPLICABILITY. (a) The
  commissioners court of a county may by order adopt a program
  requiring site construction approval for a covered facility. An
  order adopted under this section applies only in the unincorporated
  area of the county.
         (b)  A county that adopts an order under this section may:
               (1)  require the large load customer or a person acting
  on the customer's behalf to submit a pre-construction noise study
  and proof of public posting as a condition of site construction
  approval;
               (2)  condition site construction approval on
  implementation of noise mitigation measures necessary to satisfy
  Section 233.173;
               (3)  require submission and public posting of a
  post-construction noise study and any necessary corrective
  measures as a continuing condition of site construction approval;
  and
               (4)  designate an official or department to review
  applications, determine compliance, and administer and enforce
  this subchapter.
         (c)  The authority granted by this subchapter is express
  statutory authority to require the noise studies, site plans
  showing noise mitigation measures, and other documents specified by
  this subchapter. The authority is separate from, and does not
  expand, a county's general authority to regulate land use or
  zoning.
         (d)  The commissioners court may adopt reasonable
  administrative procedures and charge a fee not greater than the
  county's reasonable cost of reviewing an application, verifying
  publication, and administering this subchapter.
         Sec. 233.173.  MAXIMUM SOUND LEVELS. (a)  An order adopted
  under Section 233.172 must require that sound attributable to
  routine operation of a covered facility, measured at the nearest
  point on each affected receiving property line, not exceed the
  following maximum A-weighted sound levels:
               (1)  55 dB(A) for a property used for a dwelling or
  primarily for agricultural or rural purposes;
               (2)  60 dB(A) for a dwelling unit located within a mixed
  residential and commercial development;
               (3)  65 dB(A) for property used primarily for
  commercial, civic, or institutional purposes; and
               (4)  70 dB(A) for property used primarily for
  industrial purposes.
         (b)  The maximum sound levels under Subsection (a) apply at
  all times of day and night. A county shall classify receiving
  property according to its existing lawful use or an approved
  residential development plan, regardless of whether the property is
  subject to zoning. If more than one category applies, the more
  protective maximum sound level controls.
         (c)  In determining compliance, sound originating from
  sources not associated with the covered facility must be identified
  and excluded to the extent practicable using generally accepted
  acoustical methods. A covered facility is not required to reduce
  unrelated pre-existing ambient sound.
         (d)  This section does not apply to sound generated by
  aircraft, temporary construction or maintenance activity, or
  customer-owned backup generating equipment operating during an
  actual emergency or pursuant to a lawful direction of an electric
  utility or the independent organization described by Section
  39.151, Utilities Code. This exception does not apply to routine
  generator testing or commissioning.
         (e)  This section does not authorize a violation of a state
  or federal environmental law or a permit issued under that law.
         Sec. 233.174.  NOISE STUDY AND MEASUREMENT STANDARDS. (a)  A
  noise study required under this subchapter must be prepared or
  certified by a professional engineer described by Section
  233.171(4) and must identify the methods, equipment, locations,
  dates, operating conditions, assumptions, and results used in the
  study.
         (b)  A pre-construction noise study must:
               (1)  document existing ambient sound conditions at the
  time of the initial site construction approval application;
               (2)  model the projected sound attributable to the
  covered facility at the receiving property lines under anticipated
  routine operating conditions, including cooling, ventilation, and
  routine generator testing;
               (3)  identify each affected receiving property, the
  applicable maximum sound level under Section 233.173, and whether
  the projected sound will comply with that level; and
               (4)  identify proposed mitigation measures if the
  projected sound level would otherwise exceed the applicable
  maximum.
         (c)  A post-construction noise study must measure sound
  produced under representative operating conditions and evaluate
  compliance with Section 233.173. If the facility has not reached
  its reasonably anticipated maximum routine operating load, the
  study must also include modeling of that load.
         (d)  Except as provided by Subsection (e), measurements
  under this section must:
               (1)  use an integrating sound level meter meeting or
  exceeding ANSI S1.43-1997, Type 1, or an equivalent successor
  performance standard;
               (2)  use the FAST response setting and a 15-second
  sampling interval, except that the engineer may select a period of
  at least 10 seconds and not more than one minute if the 15-second
  interval does not adequately characterize the sound;
               (3)  include at least three samples in which the
  subject sound is identifiable and extraneous sounds that would
  materially distort the result are excluded, together with a
  background sample when practicable;
               (4)  use the arithmetic average of samples showing
  repeatability and consistency to determine the sound level for
  comparison with Section 233.173; and
               (5)  be taken at the nearest point on the receiving
  property line, with the microphone directed toward the source and
  positioned five feet above grade, unless physical circumstances
  require a documented alternative.
         (e)  For a dwelling unit in a mixed-use development,
  measurement may be made at an open window, door, or other aperture
  nearest the source, with a windscreen-equipped microphone
  positioned at the aperture and directed toward the source. This
  subsection does not authorize entry on private property without
  permission.
         Sec. 233.175.  PUBLICATION OF NOISE STUDIES. (a)  A large
  load customer subject to a county order under Section 233.172 shall
  publish a publicly accessible version of each pre-construction and
  post-construction noise study on an Internet website accessible to
  the public without charge or registration and shall provide the
  county with the Internet address and a copy of the study.
         (b)  The pre-construction noise study must be published
  before the county issues site construction approval and remain
  publicly accessible while construction is underway. The
  post-construction study must be published not later than the date
  the study is submitted to the county and remain publicly accessible
  for at least three years after publication.
         (c)  Each published study must include the applicable sound
  limits, measurement or modeling methodology, results for receiving
  properties, the engineer's conclusions, and required mitigation
  measures.  A customer may redact only information that is
  confidential under state or federal law, including qualifying
  security-sensitive or protected commercial information. A
  redaction may not conceal the sound levels, compliance findings, or
  required mitigation measures.
         (d)  A county shall make the link to each published study
  available on the county's website or, if the county does not
  maintain a website, make the study available for public inspection
  at the office of the county clerk.
         Sec. 233.176.  SITE CONSTRUCTION APPROVAL; SUBSEQUENT
  STUDY.  (a)  In a county that has adopted an order under Section
  233.172, a large load customer may not begin construction or
  expansion of a covered facility without site construction approval
  issued under this subchapter.
         (b)  The county may issue site construction approval only
  after the applicant:
               (1)  submits the pre-construction noise study required
  by Section 233.174;
               (2)  establishes that the study has been published as
  required by Section 233.175;
               (3)  demonstrates that the covered facility, with any
  mitigation measures incorporated into the site plan, is projected
  to meet Section 233.173; and
               (4)  agrees in writing to submit and publish the
  post-construction noise study and implement any required
  corrective measures as conditions of the approval.
         (c)  A site construction approval issued under this
  subchapter is expressly conditioned on timely submission and
  publication of a post-construction noise study. The customer shall
  submit the post-construction study to the county not earlier than
  one month and not later than 12 months after the facility first
  begins routine operations.
         (d)  Not later than the 30th day after the county receives a
  complete post-construction noise study, the county shall issue a
  written determination of compliance or specify deficiencies and the
  mitigation required. If compliance is shown, the county shall issue
  a final noise compliance certificate.  A facility may operate
  during the period allowed for the post-construction study subject
  to the conditions of its site construction approval and other
  applicable law.
         (e)  An order adopted under this subchapter may require an
  additional post-construction study following a material
  modification to equipment at a covered facility that the county
  reasonably determines is likely to increase sound at a receiving
  property.
         (f)  A permit or approval required under this subchapter does
  not replace any other permit required by law.  Review of a
  subdivision plat or development plan remains subject to Chapter 232
  and other applicable law.
         Sec. 233.177.  NOISE MITIGATION; ENFORCEMENT. (a) If a
  required noise study indicates that a covered facility will exceed
  or exceeds an applicable maximum sound level, the large load
  customer shall submit to the county an affidavit or certificate
  signed by the professional engineer identifying the mitigation
  measures needed for compliance.
         (b)  Mitigation measures must be identified and labeled on
  the site plan and may include acoustical barriers, mechanical
  equipment enclosures, revised equipment placement, or other
  sound-control measures.  A pre-construction plan showing projected
  exceedances may not be approved unless the proposed mitigation is
  projected to bring the facility into compliance.
         (c)  If a post-construction study shows noncompliance, the
  county shall provide written notice describing the violation and
  require a corrective action plan with a reasonable deadline, not
  later than the 90th day after notice unless an extension is
  justified in writing. The county may require an additional
  engineer-certified verification study after mitigation is
  installed.
         (d)  The county attorney, district attorney, or other
  attorney representing the county may bring an action in district
  court for injunctive relief to prevent construction without
  required site construction approval or to compel compliance with
  the publication, post-construction study, or mitigation conditions
  of an approval. A remedy affecting operation of a facility must be
  tailored to avoid interfering with a lawful electric system
  emergency instruction.
         (e)  Before revoking a site construction approval for a
  violation of this subchapter, the county shall provide written
  notice, a reasonable opportunity to cure, and an opportunity for a
  hearing before the commissioners court or a designated hearing
  officer.
         Sec. 233.178.  LIMITATIONS; OTHER LAW.  (a)  This subchapter
  does not authorize a county to adopt zoning regulations, regulate
  an electric utility's rates or interconnection service, or impose a
  condition inconsistent with a state or federal permit.
         (b)  The applicability of this subchapter to a project is
  subject to Chapter 245, including any vested rights protected by
  that chapter.
         (c)  Nothing in this subchapter limits a municipality's
  authority within its corporate limits or authorizes a county to
  require access to private property without consent or other lawful
  authority.
         SECTION 3.  The changes in law made by this Act apply only to
  construction or expansion of a covered facility for which an
  initial application for a permit for the project is filed on or
  after September 1, 2027. Construction or expansion of a covered
  facility for which the initial permit application was filed before
  that date is governed by the law in effect when the application was
  filed, and the former law is continued in effect for that purpose.
         SECTION 4.  This Act takes effect September 1, 2027.
